\begin{abstract}
This article proposes two finite-dimensional controllers for wave PDE--ODE cascade systems with distributed displacement and velocity coupling. It is shown that the closed-loop system can be exponentially stabilized under suitable spectral and controllability conditions on the open-loop ODE plant. The proposed controllers are constructed using a forwarding transformation that converts the distributed coupling into an equivalent finite-dimensional boundary input channel. Compared with existing backstepping-based designs, the proposed controllers have a much simpler structure and require only the ODE state and local boundary measurements, rather than the full distributed wave state. However, additional spectral restrictions on the open-loop plant are imposed to achieve this simplification. Moreover, an even simpler controller is obtained when the eigenvalues of the ODE plant are all at the origin. Several numerical examples are provided to illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed controllers.

\end{abstract}

Boundary control, wave equation, distributed parameter systems, finite-dimensional control, PDE--ODE cascade systems.

\section{Introduction}


Interconnections between ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and partial differential equations (PDEs) arise in various engineering systems involving flexible structures and mechanical vibrations \cite{krstic_compensating_2009,susto_control_2010,bekiaris-liberis_compensating_2010}. In particular, when the vibrations of a flexible structure are described by a wave equation and its distributed displacement and velocity affect a finite-dimensional subsystem, the resulting dynamics can be modeled as a wave PDE--ODE cascade with distributed coupling. In such systems, the control input may be applied at the boundary of the wave equation, whereas the ODE is driven by the wave state distributed over the spatial domain. In this context, we are concerned with the boundary stabilization of wave PDE--ODE cascades with distributed coupling.




Various approaches have been developed for the stabilization of PDE--ODE cascades. Backstepping has been widely employed to stabilize coupled PDE--ODE systems through infinite-dimensional transformations and boundary feedback design \cite{krstic_backstepping_2008,krstic_compensating_2009,susto_control_2010,bekiaris-liberis_compensating_2010}. More recently, Tsubakino and Krstic \cite{tsubakino_forwarding-based_2024} developed a forwarding-based controller for general linear ODE--wave cascades with distributed interconnections, allowing unstable ODE dynamics. However, these designs may require distributed PDE-state measurements and spatial integral terms in the feedback law. To overcome limitations in state availability, output-feedback controllers have also been developed using state observers and disturbance estimators \cite{than_output-feedback_2021,mei_infinite-dimensional_2023,feng_output-feedback_2024}. Although these methods reduce direct state-measurement requirements, the additional observer dynamics, particularly for infinite-dimensional systems, may increase the complexity of real-time implementation. These considerations motivate the development of stabilizing controllers with simpler online feedback structures.



To simplify the feedback implementation, Xu et al. \cite{xu_low-gain_2023} proposed a finite-dimensional feedback design for PDE--ODE cascades with distributed diffusion and counter convection. Their approach achieves exponential stabilization without requiring the full PDE state in the feedback law, at the expense of additional spectral restrictions on the ODE subsystem. This result motivates us to investigate whether a similar finite-dimensional control structure can be developed for wave PDE--ODE cascades. However, unlike diffusion equations, wave equations generally lack intrinsic energy dissipation and may possess nondecaying modes under certain boundary conditions. As a result, the stability analysis developed for diffusion PDE--ODE cascades cannot be directly applied to wave PDE--ODE cascades, since the dissipative properties underlying the original analysis are no longer available.



To address these challenges, this paper develops a finite-dimensional feedback controller for a linear ODE coupled with a one-dimensional wave equation through spatially distributed displacement and velocity terms. The wave equation satisfies a homogeneous Neumann boundary condition at $x=0$, with the control input applied at $x=D$. Motivated by existing boundary stabilization methods for wave equations \cite{krstic_output-feedback_2008,tsubakino_forwarding-based_2024}, we introduce boundary damping together with an additional stiffness feedback term to eliminate the nondecaying displacement mode and stabilize the wave subsystem. Following the finite-dimensional compensation idea in \cite{xu_low-gain_2023}, an auxiliary boundary-value problem is constructed to account for the distributed displacement and velocity coupling and determine an effective input vector $B$. The resulting controller takes the form
\begin{equation}
U(t)=-B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)-b\,u_t(D,t)-q\,u(D,t),
\label{eq:introcontroller}
\end{equation}
where $P_\varepsilon$ is the positive definite solution of a parametric algebraic Riccati equation. Unlike feedback designs requiring distributed wave-state information, the proposed controller uses only the ODE state and the wave displacement and velocity at the controlled boundary.

The main contributions of this paper, compared with relevant existing works, are summarized as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item To the best of our knowledge, this is the first attempt to develop a finite-dimensional feedback controller of the proposed structure for wave PDE--ODE cascades with spatially distributed displacement and velocity coupling. Compared with the forwarding-based design in \cite{tsubakino_forwarding-based_2024}, the proposed controller has a simpler form and does not require distributed wave-state integration in the online feedback. Unlike observer-based approaches \cite{than_output-feedback_2021,mei_infinite-dimensional_2023,feng_output-feedback_2024}, it introduces no additional observer dynamics. These advantages are obtained at the expense of more restrictive spectral conditions on the ODE subsystem.

\item A composite Lyapunov functional is developed to establish exponential stability under the proposed finite-dimensional feedback. The main difficulty lies in handling the multiple residual coupling terms arising from the distributed displacement and velocity interactions. To overcome this difficulty, the Riccati-based ODE energy is combined with the boundary-damped wave energy and suitable auxiliary terms. Through a careful selection of the functional weights and feedback parameters, the resulting cross terms are systematically estimated and absorbed into the dissipative terms, yielding a strict decay estimate for the coupled closed-loop system.

\item For the special case where the ODE matrix is nilpotent, the proposed controller admits a further simplified representation. By exploiting the nilpotent structure, the effective input vector can be expressed in a more explicit form, reducing the complexity of the offline controller design while preserving the finite-dimensional feedback structure. Numerical simulations are conducted to illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed controller and the exponential decay of the closed-loop states.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:prob} presents the problem formulation and preliminary results. Section~\ref{sec:stab} develops the proposed controllers and establishes exponential stability. Numerical examples are provided in Section~\ref{sec:num}, followed by conclusions in Section~\ref{sec:concl}.

The notation $\norm{\cdot}_2$ denotes the $L^2(0,D)$ norm, and $\dint{\cdot}$ abbreviates $\int_0^D$. For a positive definite matrix $P$, $P^{1/2}$ denotes its unique positive definite square root. \section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prob}
\subsection{Problem Formulation}
In this article, we consider the following PDE--ODE cascade system:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot{X}(t)={}&AX(t)
+\dint{B_1(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}
+\dint{B_2(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ode}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
u_{tt}(x,t)=u_{xx}(x,t),
\label{eq:wave}
\end{equation}
with boundary conditions given as follows:
\begin{equation}
u_x(0,t)=0,
\label{eq:left_boundary}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
u_x(D,t)=U(t),
\label{eq:right_boundary}
\end{equation}
where $X(t)\in\mathbb{R}^n$ is the plant state,
$U(t)\in\mathbb{R}$ is the control signal,
$u(x,t):[0,D]\times[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$
is the actuator state, and
$B_1(x),B_2(x):[0,D]\to\mathbb{R}^n$
are distributed coupling functions satisfying
\begin{equation}
\int_0^D |B_i(x)|^2\,\mathrm{d}x<+\infty,
\quad i=1,2.
\label{eq:kernel_condition}
\end{equation}
The ODE subsystem is affected by the distributed
displacement and velocity of the wave, while the
wave equation describes the actuator dynamics.
The boundary conditions are of Neumann type,
with the control signal applied at $x=D$.


The control objective is to design $U(t)$ using only $X(t)$, $u(D,t)$, and
$u_t(D,t)$ so that the closed-loop energy
\begin{equation}
\Omega(t) := \abs{X(t)}^2 + \dint{\left(u_x^2(x,t) + u_t^2(x,t)\right)
\,\mathrm{d}x} + u^2(D,t)
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
decays exponentially.


\subsection{Technical Lemmas}

The following lemmas provide useful Riccati properties
\cite{lin_low-gain_1999,xu_stabilization_2018,xu_semi-global_2019}
and Poincar\'e-type inequalities
\cite{krstic_smyshlyaev_boundary_2008}.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:riccati}
Assume $(A,B)$ is controllable and all eigenvalues of $A$ lie on the imaginary
axis. Then the parametric algebraic Riccati equation (ARE)
\begin{equation}
A^{\top}P + PA-PBB^{\top}P = -\varepsilon P
\label{eq:are}
\end{equation}
has a unique positive definite solution $P=P_\varepsilon>0$. Moreover, there
exist constants $K_P>0$ and $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
\|P_\varepsilon\|\leq K_P\varepsilon,
\qquad
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_0.
\label{eq:Pgain}
\end{equation}
If all eigenvalues of $A$ are at the origin, then, in addition,
\begin{equation}
A^{\top}P_\varepsilon A
\preceq 3n^2\varepsilon^2 P_\varepsilon.
\label{eq:riccati_nilp}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:poincare}
For every $y\in H^1(0,D)$,
\begin{equation}
\norm{y}_2^2
\leq 2D\,y^2(D)+D^2\norm{y_x}_2^2,
\label{eq:poincare}
\end{equation}
and
$\norm{y}_{L^\infty}^2
\leq 2y^2(D)+2D\norm{y_x}_2^2$.
\end{lemma}


\subsection{Offline Kernel Construction}

\paragraph{Forwarding transformation.}
Following \cite{tsubakino_forwarding-based_2024}, define the compensated state
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Z(t)={}&X(t)+\dint{g_0(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}\\
&+\dint{g(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}+b\,g(D)u(D,t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Z}
\end{equation}
The kernels are selected so that the distributed coupling is converted into an
equivalent boundary channel. Their defining identities are
\begin{equation}
g_0(x)=Ag(x)-B_2(x),
\qquad
g''(x)+B_1(x)=Ag_0(x),
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
subject to the boundary conditions
\begin{equation}
g'(0)=0,
\qquad
-g'(D)=(bA+qI)g(D),
\label{eq:kernelbc}
\end{equation}
where $b$ and $q$ are design parameters. Throughout, the effective
finite-dimensional input channel induced by the distributed PDE coupling is
\begin{equation}
B:=g(D).
\label{eq:Bdef}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Transformation result.}
Direct differentiation of \eqref{eq:Z} along the plant and wave dynamics,
together with integration by parts and the kernel identities
\eqref{eq:kernel}--\eqref{eq:kernelbc}, cancels all distributed coupling terms
and yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot Z(t)={}&AZ(t)+B\bigl(u_x(D,t)+b\,u_t(D,t)\\
&\hspace{28mm}+q\,u(D,t)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Ztarget}
\end{equation}
Thus the distributed PDE--ODE interconnection acts on the plant exactly as a
finite-dimensional boundary channel $B$.

\paragraph{Offline gain computation.}
Writing the kernel equations \eqref{eq:kernel} in the first-order form
$\zeta'=\Av\zeta+\eta$, $\zeta=(g,g')^{\top}$, with
\begin{equation}
\Av=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\A^2&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\eta(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\-AB_2(x)-B_1(x)\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:zeta_data}
\end{equation}
variation of constants combined with the boundary conditions
\eqref{eq:kernelbc} gives the endpoint gain \eqref{eq:Bdef} in closed form as
\begin{equation}
B=-\bigl(I\;\;0\bigr)e^{\Av D}\binom{I}{0}E^{-1}
\bigl(bA+qI\;\;I\bigr)\Xi+\bigl(I\;\;0\bigr)\Xi,
\label{eq:B}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\Xi=\dint{e^{\Av(D-s)}\eta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s},
\qquad
E=\bigl(bA+qI\;\;I\bigr)e^{\Av D}\binom{I}{0}.
\label{eq:Xi_E}
\end{equation}
The matrix $E$ is the only quantity that must be inverted, and the derivation
is the standard endpoint computation of forwarding designs
\cite{tsubakino_forwarding-based_2024}; it is therefore omitted. The gain
\eqref{eq:B} depends only on the plant data, so it is computed once, offline,
before the controller is switched on.

\subsection{Assumptions and Controller Design}

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:struct}
The design parameters satisfy $b>0$ and $0<q<1/D$, and the matrix $E$ in
\eqref{eq:Xi_E} is invertible.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Marginal case]
\label{ass:marginal}
All eigenvalues of $A$ lie on the imaginary axis, and the pair $(A,B)$, with
$B$ given by \eqref{eq:B}, is controllable.
\end{assumption}

For the more general nonexpansive case, let $T$ be a real similarity
transformation that separates the stable and critical spectral subspaces:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
T^{-1}AT&=\begin{pmatrix}A_s&0\\0&A_c\end{pmatrix},
&T^{-1}B&=\begin{pmatrix}B_s\\B_c\end{pmatrix},\\
\widetilde X:=T^{-1}X&=\begin{pmatrix}
\widetilde X_s\\\widetilde X_c
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:spectral_split}
\end{equation}
where $A_s$ is Hurwitz and all eigenvalues of $A_c$ lie on the imaginary axis.
The decomposition is understood in terms of real generalized spectral
subspaces, so it does not require $A$ to be diagonalizable.

\begin{assumption}[Nonexpansive case]
\label{ass:nonexp}
$\sigma(A)\subset\{s:\operatorname{Re}s\leq0\}$, and the critical pair
$(A_c,B_c)$ in \eqref{eq:spectral_split} is controllable. No controllability
condition is imposed on the Hurwitz pair $(A_s,B_s)$.
\end{assumption}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:riccati}, let $P=P_\varepsilon>0$ denote the solution of
\eqref{eq:are}. The boundary controller is
\begin{equation}
U(t) = -B^{\top}P_\varepsilon X(t) - b\,u_t(D,t) - q\,u(D,t).
\label{eq:controller}
\end{equation}
The term $-b\,u_t(D,t)$ injects boundary damping, $-q\,u(D,t)$ controls the
displacement mode associated with the Neumann boundary condition, and
$-B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)$ provides finite-dimensional stabilization of
the plant. For Assumption~\ref{ass:nonexp}, the
finite-dimensional term is replaced by the critical-subspace feedback
\begin{equation}
U_c(t)=-B_c^\top P_{c,\varepsilon}\widetilde X_c(t)
-b\,u_t(D,t)-q\,u(D,t),
\label{eq:critical_controller}
\end{equation}
where $P_{c,\varepsilon}>0$ solves the critical ARE
\begin{equation}
A_c^\top P_{c,\varepsilon}+P_{c,\varepsilon}A_c
-P_{c,\varepsilon}B_cB_c^\top P_{c,\varepsilon}
=-\varepsilon P_{c,\varepsilon}.
\label{eq:critical_are}
\end{equation}

\section{Main Results}
\label{sec:stab}

\subsection{General Marginally Stable Case}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm1}
Consider the closed-loop system consisting of \eqref{eq:ode}, \eqref{eq:wave},
the boundary conditions \eqref{eq:left_boundary} and \eqref{eq:right_boundary},
and the controller \eqref{eq:controller}, under
Assumptions~\ref{ass:struct} and~\ref{ass:marginal}. Then there exists
$\varepsilon^*>0$ such that, for every $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^*]$ and
every initial state $(X(0),u(\cdot,0),u_t(\cdot,0))\in
\mathbb{R}^n\times H^1(0,D)\times L^2(0,D)$, the closed-loop system admits a
unique solution
$(X(t),u(\cdot,t),u_t(\cdot,t))\in
C([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^n\times H^1(0,D)\times L^2(0,D))$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\Omega(t)\leq\alpha e^{-\beta t}\Omega(0)
\label{eq:stab}
\end{equation}
for some positive constants $\alpha$ and $\beta$. If, in addition,
$u(\cdot,0)\in H^2(0,D)$, $u_t(\cdot,0)\in H^1(0,D)$, and the initial data are
compatible with the boundary conditions and the controller, then the
corresponding solution is classical.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $g$, $g_0$, $B$, and $Z$ be defined by \eqref{eq:kernel}--\eqref{eq:Bdef}
and \eqref{eq:Z}, and set
\begin{equation}
\varphi(t):=Z(t)-X(t).
\label{eq:phi_def}
\end{equation}

\emph{Forwarding transformation.}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:Z} along the plant and wave dynamics, integrating the
wave term by parts twice, and using the kernel identities
\eqref{eq:kernel} and the boundary conditions \eqref{eq:left_boundary},
\eqref{eq:right_boundary}, and \eqref{eq:kernelbc}, all distributed coupling
terms cancel and the transformed dynamics reduce to \eqref{eq:Ztarget}. Under
the feedback law
\begin{equation}
u_x(D,t)=-B^{\top}P_\varepsilon X(t)-b\,u_t(D,t)-q\,u(D,t),
\label{eq:bc_cl}
\end{equation}
the transformed dynamics collapse to $\dot Z=AZ-BB^{\top}P_\varepsilon X$.
Since $X=Z-\varphi$,
\begin{equation}
\dot Z(t)
=
\bigl(A-BB^{\top}P_\varepsilon\bigr)Z(t)
+
BB^{\top}P_\varepsilon\varphi(t).
\label{eq:Zdot2}
\end{equation}
Thus the distributed coupling enters the finite-dimensional dynamics only
through the perturbation $\varphi$.

\emph{Transformation-error estimate.}
Define the wave energy
\begin{equation}
E_u(t)
=
\frac{1}{2}\dint{u_x^2(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}
+
\frac{1}{2}\dint{u_t^2(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}
+
\frac{q}{2}u^2(D,t).
\label{eq:Eu}
\end{equation}
For $u\in H^1(0,D)$, writing
$u(x,t)=u(D,t)-\int_x^D u_x(\zeta,t)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta$ and applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:poincare} gives
\begin{equation}
\norm{u(\cdot,t)}_{L^\infty}^2
\leq C_\infty E_u(t),
\qquad
\norm{u(\cdot,t)}_2^2
\leq C_0 E_u(t),
\label{eq:ubounds}
\end{equation}
where $C_\infty=4(D+q^{-1})$ and $C_0=4D(D+q^{-1})$ are independent of
$\varepsilon$. From \eqref{eq:phi_def} and \eqref{eq:Z}, the transformation
error is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\varphi(t)={}&\dint{g_0(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}
 +\dint{g(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}\\
&+bB\,u(D,t),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
and Cauchy--Schwarz together with the three-term square inequality yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\abs{\varphi(t)}^2 &\leq C_\varphi E_u(t),\\
C_\varphi
&=
3\norm{g_0}_2^2C_0
+
6\norm{g}_2^2
+
\frac{6b^2\abs{B}^2}{q}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Cphi}
\end{equation}

\emph{Finite-dimensional Lyapunov estimate.}
Let
\begin{equation}
V_1(t)=Z^\top(t)P_\varepsilon Z(t).
\label{eq:V1}
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:are} and \eqref{eq:Zdot2} gives
\begin{equation}
\dot V_1
=
-\varepsilon V_1
-\bigl(B^{\top}P_\varepsilon Z\bigr)^2
+2\bigl(B^{\top}P_\varepsilon Z\bigr)
\bigl(B^{\top}P_\varepsilon\varphi\bigr).
\label{eq:V1dot}
\end{equation}
Completing the square and using \eqref{eq:Cphi},
\begin{equation}
\dot V_1
\leq
-\varepsilon V_1
+
C_1\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2E_u,
\qquad
C_1:=\abs{B}^2C_\varphi.
\label{eq:V1dot2}
\end{equation}

\emph{Wave Lyapunov estimate.}
Choose
\begin{equation}
\frac{Dq}{2}<\kappa<\frac{1}{2},
\qquad
\delta_2=\kappa\delta_1,
\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
which is possible because $q<1/D$, and define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V_2(t)={}&E_u(t)
 +\delta_1\dint{x\,u_x(x,t)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}\\
&+\delta_2\dint{u(x,t)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:V2}
\end{equation}
For $0<\delta_1\leq(2K_\delta)^{-1}$, with
$K_\delta=D+\kappa\left(\frac{C_0}{2}+1\right)$, there holds
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
m_\delta E_u(t)\leq{}& V_2(t)\leq M_\delta E_u(t),\\
m_\delta=1-{}&\delta_1K_\delta,\qquad
M_\delta=1+\delta_1K_\delta.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:V2_equiv}
\end{equation}
Using the boundary feedback, the wave energy satisfies
$\dot E_u=-bu_t^2(D,t)-u_t(D,t)B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)$, while the two
multipliers satisfy
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dint{x\,u_xu_t\,\mathrm{d}x}
&=\frac{D}{2}\bigl(u_x^2(D,t)+u_t^2(D,t)\bigr)\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\dint{(u_x^2+u_t^2)\,\mathrm{d}x},\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dint{u\,u_t\,\mathrm{d}x}
&=\dint{(u_t^2-u_x^2)\,\mathrm{d}x}+u(D,t)u_x(D,t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mult_der}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:bc_cl} and collecting terms gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot V_2={}&
-\frac{\delta_1(1+2\kappa)}{2}\norm{u_x}_2^2
-\frac{\delta_1(1-2\kappa)}{2}\norm{u_t}_2^2\\
&-\left[b-\frac{\delta_1D(1+b^2)}{2}\right]u_t^2(D,t)\\
&-\delta_1q\left(\kappa-\frac{Dq}{2}\right)u^2(D,t)\\
&+\delta_1b(Dq-\kappa)u(D,t)u_t(D,t)\\
&-(1-\delta_1Db)u_t(D,t)B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)\\
&-\delta_1(\kappa-Dq)u(D,t)B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)\\
&+\frac{\delta_1D}{2}
\bigl(B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)\bigr)^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:V2dot}
\end{equation}
The first five terms are strictly dissipative after applying Young's inequality
to $u(D,t)u_t(D,t)$ and choosing $\delta_1$ sufficiently small. Applying
Young's inequality to the two remaining mixed boundary terms, and choosing
$\delta_1>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&b-\frac{\delta_1D(1+b^2)}{2}>0,\\
&\delta_1q\left(\kappa-\frac{Dq}{2}\right)
-\frac{\delta_1^2b^2(Dq-\kappa)^2}
{b-\frac{\delta_1D(1+b^2)}{2}}>0,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:delta_displacement}
\end{equation}
one obtains
\begin{equation}
\dot V_2\leq-c_3E_u+C_2\bigl(B^\top P_\varepsilon X\bigr)^2,
\label{eq:V2dot2}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
c_3:=\min\Biggl\{&\delta_1(1+2\kappa),\quad
\delta_1(1-2\kappa),\\
&\frac{1}{q}\left[\delta_1q\left(\kappa-\frac{Dq}{2}\right)
-\frac{\delta_1^2b^2(Dq-\kappa)^2}
{b-\frac{\delta_1D(1+b^2)}{2}}\right]\Biggr\}>0,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:c3}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_2:={}&\frac{\delta_1D}{2}
+\frac{(1-\delta_1Db)^2}
{b-\frac{\delta_1D(1+b^2)}{2}}\\
&+\frac{\delta_1^2(\kappa-Dq)^2}
{2\left[\delta_1q\left(\kappa-\frac{Dq}{2}\right)
-\frac{\delta_1^2b^2(Dq-\kappa)^2}
{b-\frac{\delta_1D(1+b^2)}{2}}\right]},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:C2}
\end{equation}
both independent of $\varepsilon$. Since $X=Z-\varphi$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(B^{\top}P_\varepsilon X\bigr)^2
&\leq 2\bigl(B^{\top}P_\varepsilon Z\bigr)^2
 +2\bigl(B^{\top}P_\varepsilon\varphi\bigr)^2\\
&\leq C_3\abs{P_\varepsilon}V_1
 +C_4\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2E_u,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sbound}
\end{equation}
where $C_3:=2\abs{B}^2$ and $C_4:=2\abs{B}^2C_\varphi$, and therefore
\begin{equation}
\dot V_2
\leq
-c_3E_u
 +C_2C_3\abs{P_\varepsilon}V_1
 +C_2C_4\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2E_u.
\label{eq:V2dot3}
\end{equation}

\emph{Combination of the estimates.}
With
\begin{equation}
V(t)=V_1(t)+\varepsilon V_2(t),
\label{eq:V}
\end{equation}
combining \eqref{eq:V1dot2} and \eqref{eq:V2dot3} gives
\begin{equation}
\dot V
\leq
-\varepsilon\bigl(1-C_2C_3\abs{P_\varepsilon}\bigr)V_1
-
\left[
\varepsilon c_3
-(C_1+\varepsilon C_2C_4)\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2
\right]E_u.
\label{eq:Vdot}
\end{equation}
By the gain bound \eqref{eq:Pgain}, it suffices to choose
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^*
=
\min\left\{
\varepsilon_0,\,
\frac{1}{2C_2C_3K_P},\,
\frac{c_3}{4C_1K_P^2},\,
\sqrt{\frac{c_3}{4C_2C_4K_P^2}}
\right\},
\label{eq:epsilon_star}
\end{equation}
with any zero-denominator restriction omitted. Then, for
$0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon^*$, \eqref{eq:Vdot} and \eqref{eq:V2_equiv} give
\begin{equation}
\dot V
\leq
-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}V_1
-\frac{\varepsilon c_3}{2}E_u
\leq
-\beta V,
\qquad
\beta
=
\min\left\{
\frac{\varepsilon}{2},
\frac{c_3}{2M_\delta}
\right\}>0.
\label{eq:Vdecay}
\end{equation}

\emph{Norm equivalence and well-posedness.}
From \eqref{eq:phi} and \eqref{eq:Cphi},
$\abs{X}^2\leq 2\abs{Z}^2+2C_\varphi E_u$ and
$\abs{Z}^2\leq 2\abs{X}^2+2C_\varphi E_u$, while
$\dint{(u_x^2+u_t^2)\,\mathrm{d}x}+u^2(D,t)\leq 2(1+q^{-1})E_u$. Since
$V_1\geq\lambda_{\min}(P_\varepsilon)\abs{Z}^2$, $V_1\leq V$, and
$E_u\leq V_2/m_\delta\leq V/(\varepsilon m_\delta)$, the bound
$m_\varepsilon\Omega\leq V$ holds with
\begin{equation}
m_\varepsilon
=\left[2\lambda_{\min}(P_\varepsilon)^{-1}
+\frac{2C_\varphi+2(1+q^{-1})}{\varepsilon m_\delta}\right]^{-1}.
\label{eq:m_epsilon}
\end{equation}
Conversely, $V_1\leq 2\abs{P_\varepsilon}\abs{X}^2
+2C_\varphi\abs{P_\varepsilon}E_u$, $\varepsilon V_2\leq\varepsilon M_\delta
E_u$, and $E_u\leq\max\{\frac12,\frac q2\}\Omega$ give $V\leq M_\varepsilon
\Omega$ with
\begin{equation}
M_\varepsilon
=2\abs{P_\varepsilon}
+\left(2C_\varphi\abs{P_\varepsilon}+\varepsilon M_\delta\right)
\max\left\{\frac12,\frac q2\right\}.
\label{eq:M_epsilon}
\end{equation}
Gronwall's inequality applied to \eqref{eq:Vdecay} therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\Omega(t)
\leq
\frac{M_\varepsilon}{m_\varepsilon}
e^{-\beta t}\Omega(0),
\label{eq:stab_proof}
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:stab} with $\alpha=M_\varepsilon/m_\varepsilon$.

Finally, the distributed maps
$u\mapsto\dint{B_1(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}$ and
$u_t\mapsto\dint{B_2(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}$ are bounded from $H^1(0,D)$ and
$L^2(0,D)$, respectively, into $\mathbb{R}^n$. Together with the dissipative
boundary feedback, this gives the standard well-posedness of the closed-loop
wave--ODE system and hence existence and uniqueness of the stated energy
solution. The higher-regularity compatibility conditions in the theorem yield
the corresponding classical solution. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Nilpotent Case}
When all eigenvalues of $A$ are at the origin, the kernel construction
simplifies drastically. Let $g$ solve
\begin{equation}
g''(x)+B_1(x)=0,
\qquad
g'(0)=0,
\qquad
-g'(D)=qg(D),
\label{eq:nilpBVP}
\end{equation}
and set $g_0(x)=-B_2(x)$. Integrating \eqref{eq:nilpBVP} gives
$g'(x)=-\int_0^x B_1(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta$; evaluating at $x=D$ and using
the boundary condition yields $qg(D)=\dint{B_1(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}$, so that the
effective endpoint gain is available in closed form as
\begin{equation}
\bar{B}=g(D)=\frac{1}{q}\dint{B_1(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}.
\label{eq:nilpB}
\end{equation}
Unlike the general case, no matrix-valued boundary-value solve is required.
The compensated state becomes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar{Z}(t)={}&X(t)-\dint{B_2(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}\\
&+\dint{g(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}+b\bar{B}u(D,t),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nilpZ}
\end{equation}
and satisfies, by the same integration-by-parts calculation,
\begin{equation}
\dot{\bar{Z}}(t)
=AX(t)+\bar{B}\bigl(u_x(D,t)+b\,u_t(D,t)+q\,u(D,t)\bigr).
\label{eq:nilpZdot}
\end{equation}
The corresponding controller is
\begin{equation}
U(t)=-\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon X(t)-b\,u_t(D,t)-q\,u(D,t),
\label{eq:nilpCtrl}
\end{equation}
where $P_\varepsilon$ is the positive definite solution of
\begin{equation}
A^\top P_\varepsilon+P_\varepsilon A
-P_\varepsilon\bar{B}\bar{B}^\top P_\varepsilon
=-\varepsilon P_\varepsilon.
\label{eq:nilp_are}
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Nilpotent case]
\label{ass:nilp}
All eigenvalues of $A$ are at the origin and the pair $(A,\bar{B})$ is
controllable, where $\bar{B}$ is given by \eqref{eq:nilpB}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm2}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:struct} and~\ref{ass:nilp}, the closed-loop system
consisting of \eqref{eq:ode}, \eqref{eq:wave}, \eqref{eq:left_boundary},
\eqref{eq:right_boundary}, and the controller \eqref{eq:nilpCtrl} admits, for
all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, a unique energy solution satisfying
\eqref{eq:stab}, with the same regularity statement as in
Theorem~\ref{thm1}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The construction of $E_u$, $V_2$, and the transformation-error estimate
\eqref{eq:Cphi} is identical to that in Theorem~\ref{thm1}, with $B$ replaced
by $\bar{B}$. Only the finite-dimensional estimate changes. From
\eqref{eq:nilpZdot} and \eqref{eq:nilpCtrl},
$\dot{\bar{Z}}=(A-\bar{B}\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon)X$, so that with
$\varphi:=\bar{Z}-X$,
\begin{equation}
\dot{\bar{Z}}
=\bigl(A-\bar{B}\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\bigr)\bar{Z}
-\bigl(A-\bar{B}\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\bigr)\varphi.
\label{eq:nilpZdot2}
\end{equation}
For $V_1=\bar{Z}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\bar{Z}$, the Riccati equation
\eqref{eq:nilp_are} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot V_1={}&-\varepsilon V_1
-\bigl(\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\bar{Z}\bigr)^2\\
&+2\bigl(\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\bar{Z}\bigr)
\bigl(\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\varphi\bigr)
-2\bar{Z}^{\top}P_\varepsilon A\varphi.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nilV1_identity}
\end{equation}
This differs from \eqref{eq:V1dot} only in the last term, which the nilpotent
estimate \eqref{eq:riccati_nilp} and Young's inequality bound by
$6n^2\varepsilon\abs{P_\varepsilon}\abs{\varphi}^2$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\dot V_1
\leq -\frac{\varepsilon}{2}V_1
+\bar C_\varphi\left(\abs{\bar{B}}^2\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2
+6n^2\varepsilon\abs{P_\varepsilon}\right)E_u,
\label{eq:nilV1_Eu}
\end{equation}
where we used $\abs{\varphi}^2\leq\bar C_\varphi E_u$ with
\begin{equation}
\bar C_\varphi
=3\norm{B_2}_2^2C_0+6\norm{g}_2^2
+\frac{6b^2\abs{\bar{B}}^2}{q}.
\label{eq:nilp_Cphi}
\end{equation}
By $\abs{P_\varepsilon}=O(\varepsilon)$, the forcing coefficient in
\eqref{eq:nilV1_Eu} is $O(\varepsilon^2)$. The wave estimate is unchanged in
form, i.e., \eqref{eq:V2dot2} with $B$ replaced by $\bar{B}$,
\begin{equation}
\dot V_2\leq-c_3E_u+\bar C_3\abs{P_\varepsilon}V_1
+\bar C_4\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2E_u,
\label{eq:nilV2}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\bar C_3=2C_2\abs{\bar{B}}^2,
\qquad
\bar C_4=2C_2\abs{\bar{B}}^2\bar C_\varphi.
\label{eq:nilp_C34}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V&=V_1+\varepsilon V_2,\qquad
\dot V
\leq -\frac{\varepsilon}{4}V_1
-\frac{\varepsilon c_3}{2}E_u
\leq-\beta_nV,\\
\beta_n&=\min\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{4},
\frac{c_3}{2M_\delta}\right\}>0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nilVdecay}
\end{equation}
The norm-equivalence and well-posedness arguments are identical to those in
Theorem~\ref{thm1}, with $B$ replaced by $\bar{B}$, so that
$\Omega(t)\leq\alpha_n e^{-\beta_n t}\Omega(0)$ for some $\alpha_n>0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Nonexpansive ODE Extension}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm3}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:struct} and~\ref{ass:nonexp}, the closed-loop
system with controller \eqref{eq:critical_controller} is exponentially stable
for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$ in the energy norm $\Omega$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The forwarding calculation \eqref{eq:Ztarget} is unchanged. In the coordinates
\eqref{eq:spectral_split}, write
$\widetilde Z=T^{-1}Z=(\widetilde Z_s,\widetilde Z_c)$ and
$\widetilde\varphi=T^{-1}\varphi=(\widetilde\varphi_s,\widetilde\varphi_c)$.
Applying the same forwarding transformation to the critical subsystem and a
standard quadratic Lyapunov function $V_s=\widetilde Z_s^{\top}P_s\widetilde
Z_s$, where $A_s^{\top}P_s+P_sA_s=-Q_s<0$, to the Hurwitz subsystem yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot V_c&\leq-\varepsilon V_c+C_c\abs{P_{c,\varepsilon}}^2E_u,\\
\dot V_s&\leq-\lambda_sV_s+C_s\abs{P_{c,\varepsilon}}V_c
+C_s\abs{P_{c,\varepsilon}}^2E_u
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:split_estimates}
\end{equation}
for constants $\lambda_s,C_c,C_s>0$ independent of $\varepsilon$, where
\eqref{eq:Cphi} is used after enlarging $C_\varphi$ by the fixed factor
$\norm{T^{-1}}^2$ to account for $\widetilde\varphi=T^{-1}\varphi$. The wave
estimate \eqref{eq:V2dot2} is unchanged. Hence
\begin{equation}
V=V_c+\varepsilon V_s+\varepsilon V_2
\label{eq:split_V}
\end{equation}
satisfies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot V
&\leq
-\varepsilon[1-(C_s+C_3)\abs{P_{c,\varepsilon}}]V_c
-\varepsilon\lambda_sV_s\\
&\quad
-\bigl[\varepsilon c_3
-(C_c+\varepsilon C_s+\varepsilon C_4)\abs{P_{c,\varepsilon}}^2\bigr]E_u,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:split_Vdot}
\end{equation}
which, by $\abs{P_{c,\varepsilon}}\leq K_P\varepsilon$, reduces for all
sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$ to
\begin{equation}
\dot V
\leq
-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}V_c
-\varepsilon\lambda_sV_s
-\frac{\varepsilon c_3}{2}E_u
\leq-\beta V.
\label{eq:split_Vdecay}
\end{equation}
The norm equivalence and well-posedness follow exactly as in
Theorem~\ref{thm1} and are omitted.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theoretical Boundary and Implementation Remarks}
\begin{remark}
Compared with backstepping designs for wave PDE--ODE cascades, the proposed
feedback uses only $X(t)$, $u(D,t)$, and $u_t(D,t)$ online, the full-domain
kernel being used offline to compute $B$ or $\bar{B}$. The price of this
simpler online structure is the spectral restriction in
Assumption~\ref{ass:marginal} and the controllability requirement on the
critical pair in Assumption~\ref{ass:nonexp}.
\end{remark}

\section{Numerical Simulation}
\label{sec:num}
The wave PDE is discretized by a uniform method-of-lines scheme with $N=200$
spatial intervals and a classical fourth-order Runge--Kutta time integrator
($\Delta t=5\times10^{-4}$). The Neumann boundary conditions are implemented
using ghost points, and the distributed and kernel integrals are evaluated by
the trapezoidal rule. Throughout, $D=1$, $t\in[0,50]$, and $u_t(x,0)=0$. The
gain parameter is chosen small enough to be consistent with the sufficient
condition of Theorem~\ref{thm1}.

\subsection{Example 1: General Marginally Stable Case}
\label{ex1}
Consider the cascade \eqref{eq:ode}--\eqref{eq:wave} with
\begin{equation}
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1\\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad b = 1,\qquad q = 0.4,
\label{eq:ex1A}
\end{equation}
and smooth kernels
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
B_1(x) &= e^{-30(x-0.7)^2}\begin{pmatrix} 1\\ 0.4 \end{pmatrix},\\
B_2(x) &= e^{-20(x-0.35)^2}\begin{pmatrix} 1\\ 0.25 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ex1B}
\end{equation}
The profile $B_1$ is concentrated near the actuated boundary so that the
displacement channel regulates the oscillator directly, while $B_2$ is centered
in the interior to keep the velocity coupling genuinely distributed. With the
initial data
\begin{equation}
X(0) = \begin{pmatrix} 0.40\\ -0.2301 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
u(x,0) = 0.08\cos\frac{\pi x}{2D},
\label{eq:ex1init}
\end{equation}
the matrix $E$ in \eqref{eq:Xi_E} is invertible, and \eqref{eq:B} gives the
effective gain
\begin{equation}
B = \begin{pmatrix} 0.2235\\ -0.5423 \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:ex1Bgain}
\end{equation}
Since the eigenvalues of $A$ are $\pm i$ and
$\operatorname{rank}\left[B\;\;AB\right] = 2$,
Assumption~\ref{ass:marginal} holds. Choosing the gain parameter
$\varepsilon = 0.12$ in \eqref{eq:are} gives the gain vector
\begin{equation}
B^{\top}P_{0.12} = \left(0.1786,\; -0.3689\right).
\label{eq:ex1K}
\end{equation}
The implemented controller is therefore
\begin{equation}
U(t) = -0.1786\,x_1(t) + 0.3689\,x_2(t) - u_t(D,t) - 0.4\,u(D,t).
\label{eq:ex1Ctrl}
\end{equation}


Figure~\ref{fig:ex1}(a) shows that both ODE states converge to the origin under
the controller \eqref{eq:ex1Ctrl}, and Figure~\ref{fig:ex1}(b) shows the decay
of the distributed actuator state. Hence the complete PDE--ODE cascade is
stabilized in the general marginally stable case, in agreement with
Theorem~\ref{thm1}.

\subsection{Example 2: Nilpotent Case with Baseline Comparison}
\label{ex2}
To illustrate the simplified zero-eigenvalue design, consider the
two-dimensional chain
\begin{equation}
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1\\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad b = 1,\qquad q = 0.5,
\label{eq:ex2A}
\end{equation}
with constant coupling
\begin{equation}
B_1(x) = \begin{pmatrix} 0\\ 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad B_2(x) = 0.
\label{eq:ex2B}
\end{equation}
All eigenvalues of $A$ are at the origin, so this example falls into the
framework of \eqref{eq:nilpBVP}--\eqref{eq:nilpCtrl}. Since $q = 0.5$, the
explicit gain \eqref{eq:nilpB} becomes
\begin{equation}
\bar{B} = \frac{1}{q}\dint{B_1(x)\,\mathrm{d}x} =
\begin{pmatrix} 0\\ 2 \end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:ex2Bbar}
\end{equation}
and choosing $\varepsilon = 0.20$ gives
\begin{equation}
\bar{B}^{\top}P_{0.20} = \left(0.0200,\; 0.2000\right),
\label{eq:ex2K}
\end{equation}
so the implemented controller is
\begin{equation}
U(t) = -\left(0.0200,\; 0.2000\right)X(t) - u_t(D,t) - 0.5\,u(D,t).
\label{eq:ex2Ctrl}
\end{equation}
The initial conditions are
\begin{equation}
X(0) = \begin{pmatrix} 0.30\\ -0.20 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
u(x,0) = 0.04\cos\frac{\pi x}{2D}.
\label{eq:ex2init}
\end{equation}


Figure~\ref{fig:ex2} compares both the ODE and PDE responses with and without
the finite-dimensional feedback term. Boundary damping alone cannot stabilize the
nilpotent ODE chain, whereas the proposed controller drives the ODE states and
the wave displacement to the origin. This confirms the effectiveness of the
simplified controller \eqref{eq:nilpCtrl} in the nilpotent case.

\subsection{Example 3: Convergence Rate Versus the Gain Parameter}
\label{ex3}
The finite-dimensional design yields a structurally simple controller, but
its convergence can be slow, since the sufficient bound $\varepsilon^*$ in
\eqref{eq:epsilon_star} is typically conservative. To expose this trade-off we
reuse the plant, kernels, and initial data of Example~\ref{ex1} and sweep
the gain parameter:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon \in \{0.02,\; 0.05,\; 0.08,\; 0.12,\; 0.20,\; 0.30\}.
\label{eq:ex3eps}
\end{equation}
All remaining controller and discretization parameters are unchanged, and each
value of $\varepsilon$ is inserted into \eqref{eq:are} to obtain the
corresponding gain $B^{\top}P_\varepsilon$.


Figure~\ref{fig:ex3}(a) shows that every tested value stabilizes the cascade,
but the decay becomes markedly slower as $\varepsilon$ decreases: the smaller
the gain the sufficient condition demands, the longer the transient.
Figure~\ref{fig:ex3}(b) quantifies this over the whole sweep: the terminal norm
falls by almost four orders of magnitude as $\varepsilon$ grows from $0.02$ to
$0.20$, then deteriorates at $\varepsilon=0.30$, where the design leaves the
range covered by the sufficient condition. This conservatism of small
parametric gains is also reported for parabolic cascades in
\cite{xu_low-gain_2023}, and it indicates that $\varepsilon$ should be tuned
rather than merely set small.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}
This paper studied finite-dimensional stabilization for wave PDE--ODE
cascade systems
with distributed displacement and velocity coupling. A forwarding-based
transformation reduces the distributed input dynamics to a
finite-dimensional design problem with an explicit effective input vector
$B$, yielding the simple controller $U(t) = -B^{\top}P_\varepsilon X(t) - b\,u_t(D,t) - q\,u(D,t)$ that
combines a finite-dimensional stabilizing term with boundary damping and
stiffness; in the nilpotent case the gain admits the explicit formula
\eqref{eq:nilpB}. Under a spectral assumption on the ODE dynamics the closed loop
is exponentially stable in a natural energy norm, as proved by a
Lyapunov functional combining the Riccati-based ODE term with the damped wave
energy. The numerical study confirms the theory and shows that the gain
parameter trades convergence speed against robustness, so it should be tuned
rather than merely set small. Compared with backstepping designs, the proposed
feedback is much simpler online; the price is the stronger open-loop
spectral assumption. Future work includes output-feedback designs and extensions to more
general hyperbolic actuator dynamics.
