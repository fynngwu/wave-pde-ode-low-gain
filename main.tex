\begin{abstract}
This article proposes two low-gain boundary controllers for wave PDE--ODE cascade systems with distributed displacement and velocity coupling. It is shown that the closed-loop system can be exponentially stabilized under suitable spectral conditions on the open-loop ODE plant. The proposed controllers are developed using a forwarding-based design, which transforms the distributed PDE--ODE interconnection into an equivalent finite-dimensional boundary channel and enables a low-gain stabilization strategy. Compared with existing backstepping-based designs, the proposed controllers have a much simpler structure and require only the finite-dimensional plant state and local boundary measurements for online implementation. This simplicity is achieved at the cost of additional spectral restrictions on the open-loop plant. An even simpler controller is obtained for the nilpotent case. The proposed approach also accommodates distributed coupling kernels with square-integrable regularity. Several numerical simulations are provided to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed controllers.

\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Boundary control, wave equation, distributed parameter systems, low-gain compensation, PDE--ODE cascade systems.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Many control systems deliver the controller command to the ODE
plant through a non-ideal actuator path, whose finite length,
elasticity, or inertia cannot be neglected. The command must first
propagate through the actuator, and hence acts on the plant through a
distributed state rather than at a single point. Wave equations
provide a natural model for such actuator dynamics, since
propagation and energy storage are dominant \cite{evans_pde_2010}, \cite{curtain_zwart_introduction_1995}.
This viewpoint is closely related to the classical compensation of
input delays \cite{artstein_linear-systems_1982, richard_time-delay_2003, krstic_lyapunov_2008, krstic_lyapunov_2010, bekiaris-liberis_lyapunov_2011}, but it retains more of the
physical actuator dynamics than a pure delay representation.

Boundary control of wave equations and PDE backstepping have been
extensively studied \cite{krstic_output-feedback_2008, krstic_smyshlyaev_boundary_2008, vazquez_backstepping_2026, krstic_backstepping_2008}. For a wave PDE entering the
plant, an explicit compensator was first constructed in \cite{krstic_compensating_2009}
for a pointwise interconnection and extended in \cite{bekiaris-liberis_compensating_2010, bekiaris-liberis_compensating_2011} to
distributed in-domain couplings. However, it is noted from these
works that such backstepping controllers tend to be algebraically
involved and typically require full distributed-state information,
since the cascade is not in a strict-feedback form and the wave
subsystem has infinitely many imaginary-axis modes. Recent work has
moved wave and hyperbolic PDE--ODE cascades toward output feedback,
disturbances, and delays \cite{than_output-feedback_2021, mei_infinite-dimensional_2023, feng_output-feedback_2024, wang_delay-adaptive_2024}, usually at the price
of adding observer, disturbance-estimator, or adaptive modules.

On the other hand, low-gain feedback provides simple controllers for
linear systems with input saturation and/or input delays
\cite{lin_asymptotic_2007, zhou_stabilization_2012, lin_semiglobal_1993, teel_semiglobal_1995, lin_low-gain_1999, xu_stabilization_2018, xu_semi-global_2019, xu_stability_2020} under additional spectral assumptions on the
open-loop plant. Most relevant to the distributed-effect mechanism
considered here, low-gain compensation was established in \cite{xu_low-gain_2023}
for diffusion--convection PDE--ODE cascades, where the actuator
dynamics are modeled by parabolic equations, and \cite{xu_backstepping-forwarding_2024} shows
how distributed interconnections increase the complexity of both the
controller and the proof. This inspires us to consider whether a
similarly simple low-gain design exists for a \emph{conservative
wave} actuator with distributed displacement and velocity coupling,
whose hyperbolic nature and infinitely many imaginary-axis modes
preclude a direct extension of the parabolic results.

In the cascade considered here, the boundary input $U(t)$ is applied
at the right endpoint $x = D$ of a one-dimensional wave actuator
$u(x,t)$, the left endpoint $x = 0$ carries a free (Neumann)
boundary condition $u_x(0,t) = 0$, and the plant state $X(t)$ is
driven by the spatially integrated effects of the wave displacement
and velocity through $\dint{B_1(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}$ and
$\dint{B_2(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}$. The plant is therefore coupled
to the whole actuator profile rather than to a single boundary trace.
Because the wave equation is conservative in the interior, the
boundary feedback must simultaneously inject damping and stiffness at
the controlled endpoint. The proposed controller is
\begin{equation}
U(t)=-B^{\top}P_\varepsilon X(t)-b\,u_t(D,t)-q\,u(D,t).
\label{eq:introcontroller}
\end{equation}
The first term provides low-gain finite-dimensional stabilization, while
the latter two terms provide boundary damping and stiffness.

In this article, we propose two low-gain boundary controllers for
wave PDE--ODE cascade systems with distributed displacement and
velocity coupling. It is shown that the closed-loop system can be
exponentially stabilized with the proposed controllers as long as the
open-loop ODE plant is not exponentially unstable. In contrast to the
backstepping designs \cite{krstic_compensating_2009}, \cite{bekiaris-liberis_compensating_2010}, the proposed controllers
are structurally much simpler, requiring only the finite-dimensional
state and two local boundary traces online, while the effective gain
is obtained from an offline kernel boundary-value problem; an even
simpler controller is constructed for the nilpotent case. Main
contributions of this work, compared with relevant existing works,
can be summarized as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item To the best of the authors' knowledge, this is the first
application of the low-gain feedback approach to boundary control
problems with wave PDE actuator dynamics and distributed displacement
and velocity coupling.
\item Compared with the backstepping designs \cite{krstic_compensating_2009}, \cite{bekiaris-liberis_compensating_2010},
the proposed controller is structurally simpler: the online feedback
law uses only the finite-dimensional state $X(t)$ and the local
boundary traces $u(D,t)$ and $u_t(D,t)$, while the effective gain is
obtained from an offline kernel boundary-value problem. The price is
the additional spectral restriction in Assumption~\ref{ass1}.
\item A new composite Lyapunov functional is constructed for the full PDE–ODE cascade by combining the Riccati energy with a wave energy and using a small‑gain argument to dominate cross‑terms, directly yielding exponential stability in the natural norm with only square‑integrable coupling kernels; simulations verify the design.
\end{enumerate}

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:prob} formulates
the problem, recalls the low-gain Riccati framework, and constructs the offline
kernel.
Section~\ref{sec:stab} states the main stability theorem and gives its proof.
Section~\ref{sec:num} reports numerical simulations, and
Section~\ref{sec:concl} concludes.

denote the absolute value of a real scalar, the magnitude of a complex
number, the Euclidean length of a vector, and the operator 2-norm of a
matrix. Given a positive definite matrix $P \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$,
the symbol $P^{\frac{1}{2}}$ stands for the unique positive definite square
root of $P$. The letters $I$ and $0$ denote, respectively, the identity
matrix and the zero matrix, both of compatible size. $\norm{\cdot}_2$
represents the standard $L^2(0,D)$ norm, and $\dint{\cdot}$ abbreviates the
integral over $[0,D]$. For a function $u(x,t)$ smooth in both of its
variables, we write $u_x$ and $u_{xx}$ for its first- and second-order
partial derivatives with respect to $x$, and $u_t$ for its first-order
partial derivative with respect to $t$.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prob}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Consider $x \in [0, D]$ and $t \geq 0$, and the system
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot{X}(t)={}&AX(t)+\dint{B_1(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}\\
&+\dint{B_2(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ode}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
u_{tt}(x,t) = u_{xx}(x,t),
\label{eq:wave}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
u_x(0,t)=0,
\label{eq:left_boundary}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
u_x(D,t)=U(t),
\label{eq:right_boundary}
\end{equation}
where $X(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the plant state, $A \in
\mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, $U(t) \in \mathbb{R}$ is the boundary control applied
at $x = D$, and $u \colon [0,D]\times[0,\infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ is the wave
state. The coupling kernels satisfy
\begin{equation}
B_1,B_2\in L^2(0,D;\mathbb{R}^n).
\label{eq:reg}
\end{equation}
The initial data are
\begin{equation}
X(0)=X_0,\qquad u(\cdot,0)=u_0,\qquad u_t(\cdot,0)=u_1,
\label{eq:initial}
\end{equation}
with $X_0\in\mathbb{R}^n$, $u_0\in H^1(0,D)$, and
$u_1\in L^2(0,D)$ for energy solutions. Higher-regularity data are
required to satisfy the corresponding boundary compatibility conditions
when a classical solution is considered.
Equation~\eqref{eq:ode} is an $n$-dimensional linear ODE driven by the
distributed effects of the wave displacement and velocity; the wave equation
\eqref{eq:wave} models the actuator dynamics, with the boundary conditions
in \eqref{eq:left_boundary} and \eqref{eq:right_boundary}. The control objective is to design $U(t)$ using only
$X(t)$, $u(D,t)$, and $u_t(D,t)$. Define the closed-loop energy
\begin{equation}
\Omega(t) := \abs{X(t)}^2 + \dint{\left(u_x^2(x,t) + u_t^2(x,t)\right)
\,\mathrm{d}x} + u^2(D,t)
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
The control objective is to establish exponential decay of $\Omega$.

\subsection{Technical Lemmas}
This section recalls the low-gain Riccati properties and the Poincar\'e-type
estimate used in the stability analysis.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:lowgain}
Assume $(A,B)$ is controllable and all eigenvalues of $A$ lie on the imaginary
axis. Then the parametric algebraic Riccati equation (ARE)
\begin{equation}
A^{\top}P + PA-PBB^{\top}P = -\varepsilon P
\label{eq:are}
\end{equation}
has a unique positive definite solution $P=P_\varepsilon$. Moreover,
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\lim_{\varepsilon \to 0^+} P_\varepsilon = 0$ and
$\frac{\mathrm{d}P_\varepsilon}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon} > 0$ for all
$\varepsilon > 0$;
\item there exists $\vartheta_c>0$ such that $\lim_{\varepsilon \to 0^+}
\varepsilon^{-1}P_\varepsilon = \vartheta_c$;
\item if all eigenvalues of $A$ are at the origin, then $A^{\top}P_\varepsilon A
\preceq 3n^2\varepsilon^2 P_\varepsilon$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, $P_\varepsilon=O(\varepsilon)$ as
$\varepsilon\to0^+$. This estimate is the key small-gain property used
below.
These standard properties are stated without proof; see
\cite{lin_low-gain_1999,xu_stabilization_2018}, and, for item 3,
\cite{xu_semi-global_2019}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:poincare}
For every $y\in H^1(0,D)$,
\begin{equation}
\norm{y}_2^2
\leq 2D\,y^2(D)+D^2\norm{y_x}_2^2,
\label{eq:poincare}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\norm{y}_{L^\infty}^2
\leq 2y^2(D)+2D\norm{y_x}_2^2.
\label{eq:poincare_inf}
\end{equation}
These classical estimates are stated without proof; see, e.g.,
\cite{krstic_smyshlyaev_boundary_2008}.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Offline Kernel Construction}
\paragraph{Forwarding transformation.}
Following \cite{tsubakino_forwarding-based_2024}, define the compensated state
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Z(t)={}&X(t)+\dint{g_0(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}\\
&+\dint{g(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}+b\,g(D)u(D,t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Z}
\end{equation}
The kernels are selected so that the distributed coupling is transformed
into an equivalent boundary channel. Their defining identities are
\begin{equation}
g_0(x)=Ag(x)-B_2(x),
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
g''(x)+B_1(x)=Ag_0(x).
\label{eq:kernel_identity}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Kernel boundary conditions.}
The boundary conditions for the kernel are
\begin{equation}
g'(0)=0,
\qquad
-g'(D)=(bA+qI)g(D),
\label{eq:kernelbc}
\end{equation}
with design parameters
\begin{equation}
b>0,\qquad 0<q<\frac{1}{D}.
\label{eq:gains}
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{equation}
B:=g(D).
\label{eq:Bdef}
\end{equation}
The vector $B$ acts as the effective finite-dimensional input channel
induced by the distributed PDE coupling.

\paragraph{Transformation result.}
Using the kernel identities and the plant and wave boundary conditions, the
transformed dynamics reduce to
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot Z(t)={}&AZ(t)+B\bigl(u_x(D,t)+b\,u_t(D,t)\\
&\hspace{28mm}+q\,u(D,t)\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Ztarget}
\end{equation}
Thus, the distributed PDE--ODE interconnection is represented by the
finite-dimensional boundary channel $B$.

\paragraph{Offline gain computation.}
For offline computation, write the kernel equations as
\begin{equation}
\zeta(x)=\begin{pmatrix}g(x)\\g'(x)\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\zeta'(x)=\Av\,\zeta(x)+\eta(x),
\label{eq:zeta}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\Av=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\A^2&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\eta(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\-AB_2(x)-B_1(x)\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:zeta_data}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
\Xi:=\dint{e^{\Av(D-s)}\eta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s},
\label{eq:Xi}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
E := \bigl(bA + qI\;\; I\bigr)e^{\Av D}\binom{I}{0},
\label{eq:E}
\end{equation}
The endpoint gain can then be computed from the following proposition.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop1}
If $E$ is invertible, then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
B={}&-\bigl(I\;\;0\bigr)e^{\Av D}\binom{I}{0}E^{-1}\\
&\quad\times\bigl(bA+qI\;\;I\bigr)\Xi\\
&\quad+\bigl(I\;\;0\bigr)\Xi.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:B}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop1}]
Variation of constants gives
\begin{equation}
\zeta(D)=e^{\Av D}\binom{I}{0}g(0)+\Xi.
\label{eq:zeta_endpoint}
\end{equation}
Using the second condition in \eqref{eq:kernelbc} gives
\begin{equation}
g(0)=-E^{-1}\bigl(bA+qI\;\;I\bigr)\Xi.
\label{eq:g0_endpoint}
\end{equation}
Substitution into $B=(I\;\;0)\zeta(D)$ yields \eqref{eq:B}.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass1}
The following conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $b>0$ and $0<q<1/D$;
\item $E$ in \eqref{eq:E} is invertible;
\item the pair $(A,B)$, with $B$ given by \eqref{eq:B}, is controllable;
\item all eigenvalues of $A$ lie on the imaginary axis.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

For the more general nonexpansive case, let $T$ be a real similarity
transformation that separates the stable and critical spectral subspaces:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
T^{-1}AT&=\begin{pmatrix}A_s&0\\0&A_c\end{pmatrix},
&T^{-1}B&=\begin{pmatrix}B_s\\B_c\end{pmatrix},\\
\widetilde X:=T^{-1}X&=\begin{pmatrix}
\widetilde X_s\\\widetilde X_c
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:spectral_split}
\end{equation}
where $A_s$ is Hurwitz and all eigenvalues of $A_c$ lie on the imaginary
axis. The decomposition is understood in terms of real generalized spectral
subspaces, so it does not require $A$ to be diagonalizable.

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass3}
The conditions $b>0$, $0<q<1/D$, and invertibility of $E$ in
Assumption~\ref{ass1} hold, $\sigma(A)\subset\{s:\operatorname{Re}s\leq0\}$,
and the critical pair $(A_c,B_c)$ in \eqref{eq:spectral_split} is
controllable. No controllability condition is imposed on the Hurwitz pair
$(A_s,B_s)$.
\end{assumption}

\paragraph{Feedback.}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:lowgain}, let $P=P_\varepsilon>0$ denote the solution of
\eqref{eq:are}.
The boundary controller is
\begin{equation}
U(t) = -B^{\top}P_\varepsilon X(t) - b\,u_t(D,t) - q\,u(D,t).
\label{eq:controller}
\end{equation}
The term $-b\,u_t(D,t)$ injects boundary damping. The term
$-q\,u(D,t)$ controls the displacement mode associated with the Neumann
boundary condition. The term $-B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)$ provides low-gain
stabilization of the finite-dimensional subsystem.

For Assumption~\ref{ass3}, replace the finite-dimensional term by the
critical-subspace feedback
\begin{equation}
U_c(t)=-B_c^\top P_{c,\varepsilon}\widetilde X_c(t)
-b\,u_t(D,t)-q\,u(D,t),
\label{eq:critical_controller}
\end{equation}
where $P_{c,\varepsilon}>0$ solves
\begin{equation}
A_c^\top P_{c,\varepsilon}+P_{c,\varepsilon}A_c
-P_{c,\varepsilon}B_cB_c^\top P_{c,\varepsilon}
=-\varepsilon P_{c,\varepsilon}.
\label{eq:critical_are}
\end{equation}

\section{Main Results}
\label{sec:stab}

\subsection{General Marginally Stable Case}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm1}
Consider the closed-loop system consisting of \eqref{eq:ode}, \eqref{eq:wave},
the boundary conditions \eqref{eq:left_boundary} and \eqref{eq:right_boundary}, and the controller
\eqref{eq:controller}, under Assumption~\ref{ass1}. Then there exists
$\varepsilon^*>0$ such that, for every $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^*]$ and
every initial state $(X(0),u(\cdot,0),u_t(\cdot,0))\in
\mathbb{R}^n\times H^1(0,D)\times L^2(0,D)$, the closed-loop system admits a
unique solution
$(X(t),u(\cdot,t),u_t(\cdot,t))\in
C([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^n\times H^1(0,D)\times L^2(0,D))$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\Omega(t)\leq\alpha e^{-\beta t}\Omega(0),
\label{eq:stab}
\end{equation}
for some positive constants $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

Moreover, if $u(\cdot,0)\in H^2(0,D)$ and $u_t(\cdot,0)\in H^1(0,D)$, and the
initial data are compatible with the boundary conditions and the controller,
then the corresponding solution is classical.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $g$, $g_0$, $B$, and $Z$ be defined by
\eqref{eq:kernel}--\eqref{eq:Bdef} and \eqref{eq:Z}, respectively. Define
\begin{equation}
\varphi(t):=Z(t)-X(t).
\label{eq:phi_def}
\end{equation}

\emph{Forwarding transformation.}
Differentiating the forwarding transformation and substituting the plant and
wave dynamics give
\begin{align}
\dot Z(t)
&= AX(t) + \dint{B_1(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}\nonumber\\
&\quad + \dint{B_2(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}
 + \dint{g_0(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}\nonumber\\
&\quad + \dint{g(x)u_{xx}(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}
 + bB\,u_t(D,t).
\label{eq:Zdot}
\end{align}
Integrating the wave term by parts twice yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dint{g(x)u_{xx}(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}
={}&B\,u_x(D,t)-g'(D)u(D,t)\\
&+\dint{g''(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ibp}
\end{equation}
Using the kernel identities in \eqref{eq:kernel}--\eqref{eq:kernel_identity}
and the boundary conditions in \eqref{eq:left_boundary}, \eqref{eq:right_boundary}, and \eqref{eq:kernelbc},
the distributed coupling terms cancel and the transformed dynamics reduce to
\begin{equation}
\dot Z(t)
=
AZ(t)+B\bigl(u_x(D,t)+b\,u_t(D,t)+q\,u(D,t)\bigr).
\label{eq:Zdot_final}
\end{equation}
Under the feedback law,
\begin{equation}
u_x(D,t)
=
-B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)-b\,u_t(D,t)-q\,u(D,t),
\label{eq:bc_cl}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\dot Z(t)=AZ(t)-BB^\top P_\varepsilon X(t).
\label{eq:Zdot_feedback}
\end{equation}
Since $X=Z-\varphi$,
\begin{equation}
\dot Z(t)
=
(A-BB^\top P_\varepsilon)Z(t)
+
BB^\top P_\varepsilon\varphi(t).
\label{eq:Zdot2}
\end{equation}
Thus, after the forwarding transformation, the distributed coupling enters the
finite-dimensional dynamics only through the perturbation $\varphi$.

\emph{Transformation-error estimate.}
Define the wave energy
\begin{equation}
E_u(t)
=
\frac{1}{2}\dint{u_x^2(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}
+
\frac{1}{2}\dint{u_t^2(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}
+
\frac{q}{2}u^2(D,t).
\label{eq:Eu}
\end{equation}
For $u\in H^1(0,D)$,
\begin{equation}
u(x,t)=u(D,t)-\int_x^D u_x(\zeta,t)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.
\label{eq:trace_representation}
\end{equation}
Consequently, Lemma~\ref{lem:poincare} gives
\begin{equation}
\norm{u(\cdot,t)}_{L^\infty}^2
\leq C_\infty E_u(t),
\qquad
\norm{u(\cdot,t)}_2^2
\leq C_0 E_u(t),
\label{eq:ubounds}
\end{equation}
where, for example, $C_\infty=4(D+q^{-1})$ and
$C_0=4D(D+q^{-1})$ are independent of $\varepsilon$.
From the definition of $\varphi$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\varphi(t)={}&\dint{g_0(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}
 +\dint{g(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}\\
&+bB\,u(D,t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
Cauchy--Schwarz and the three-term square inequality yield
\begin{equation}
\abs{\varphi(t)}^2
\leq
C_\varphi E_u(t),
\label{eq:phi_Eu}
\end{equation}
where one may take
\begin{equation}
C_\varphi
=
3\norm{g_0}_2^2C_0
+
6\norm{g}_2^2
+
\frac{6b^2\abs{B}^2}{q}.
\label{eq:Cphi}
\end{equation}

\emph{Finite-dimensional Lyapunov estimate.}
Let
\begin{equation}
V_1(t)=Z^\top(t)P_\varepsilon Z(t).
\label{eq:V1}
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:are} and \eqref{eq:Zdot2} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot V_1
={}&-\varepsilon V_1
-\bigl(B^\top P_\varepsilon Z\bigr)^2\\
&+2\bigl(B^\top P_\varepsilon Z\bigr)
\bigl(B^\top P_\varepsilon\varphi\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:V1dot}
\end{equation}
Completing the square and using \eqref{eq:phi_Eu} gives
\begin{equation}
\dot V_1
\leq
-\varepsilon V_1
+
C_1\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2E_u,
\qquad
C_1:=\abs{B}^2C_\varphi.
\label{eq:V1dot2}
\end{equation}

\emph{Wave Lyapunov estimate.}
Choose
\begin{equation}
\frac{Dq}{2}<\kappa<\frac{1}{2},
\qquad
\delta_2=\kappa\delta_1,
\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V_2(t)={}&E_u(t)
 +\delta_1\dint{x\,u_x(x,t)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}\\
&+\delta_2\dint{u(x,t)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:V2}
\end{equation}
The interval in \eqref{eq:kappa} is nonempty because $q<1/D$. For
sufficiently small $\delta_1>0$, there exist constants $m_\delta,M_\delta>0$
such that
\begin{equation}
m_\delta E_u(t)\leq V_2(t)\leq M_\delta E_u(t).
\label{eq:V2_equiv}
\end{equation}
Indeed, Cauchy--Schwarz and Young's inequality give
\begin{equation}
\left|\delta_1\dint{x\,u_xu_t\,\mathrm{d}x}
 +\delta_2\dint{u\,u_t\,\mathrm{d}x}\right|
\leq \delta_1K_\delta E_u(t),
\label{eq:V2_cross_bound}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
K_\delta=D+\kappa\left(\frac{C_0}{2}+1\right).
\label{eq:Kdelta}
\end{equation}
Thus, for $0<\delta_1\leq(2K_\delta)^{-1}$, one may take
\begin{equation}
m_\delta=1-\delta_1K_\delta,
\qquad
M_\delta=1+\delta_1K_\delta.
\label{eq:mdelta_Mdelta}
\end{equation}
Differentiating the wave energy and using the boundary feedback gives
\begin{equation}
\dot E_u(t)
=
-bu_t^2(D,t)
-u_t(D,t)B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t).
\label{eq:energy_der2}
\end{equation}
The two multiplier derivatives are
\begin{align}
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dint{x\,u_xu_t\,\mathrm{d}x}
&=
\frac{D}{2}\bigl(u_x^2(D,t)+u_t^2(D,t)\bigr)\nonumber\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\dint{(u_x^2+u_t^2)\,\mathrm{d}x},
\label{eq:mult_der}\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dint{u\,u_t\,\mathrm{d}x}
&=
\dint{(u_t^2-u_x^2)\,\mathrm{d}x}\nonumber\\
&\quad+u(D,t)u_x(D,t).
\label{eq:mix_der}
\end{align}
Substituting the boundary condition into these identities and collecting the
terms gives
\begin{align}
\dot V_2
={}&
-\frac{\delta_1(1+2\kappa)}{2}\norm{u_x}_2^2\nonumber\\
&-\frac{\delta_1(1-2\kappa)}{2}\norm{u_t}_2^2\nonumber\\
&-\left[b-\frac{\delta_1D(1+b^2)}{2}\right]u_t^2(D,t)\nonumber\\
&-\delta_1q\left(\kappa-\frac{Dq}{2}\right)u^2(D,t)\nonumber\\
&+\delta_1b(Dq-\kappa)u(D,t)u_t(D,t)\nonumber\\
&-(1-\delta_1Db)u_t(D,t)B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)\nonumber\\
&-\delta_1(\kappa-Dq)u(D,t)B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)\nonumber\\
&+\frac{\delta_1D}{2}
\bigl(B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)\bigr)^2.
\label{eq:V2dot}
\end{align}
The first five terms are strictly dissipative after applying Young's
inequality to $u(D,t)u_t(D,t)$ and choosing $\delta_1$ sufficiently small.
Applying Young's inequality to the two remaining mixed boundary terms gives
the following explicit sufficient constants. Choose $\delta_1>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
b-\frac{\delta_1D(1+b^2)}{2}>0,
\label{eq:delta_condition}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\delta_1q\left(\kappa-\frac{Dq}{2}\right)
-\frac{\delta_1^2b^2(Dq-\kappa)^2}
{b-\frac{\delta_1D(1+b^2)}{2}}>0.
\label{eq:delta_displacement}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
c_3:=\min\Biggl\{&\delta_1(1+2\kappa),\quad
\delta_1(1-2\kappa),\\
&\frac{1}{q}\left[\delta_1q\left(\kappa-\frac{Dq}{2}\right)
-\frac{\delta_1^2b^2(Dq-\kappa)^2}
{b-\frac{\delta_1D(1+b^2)}{2}}\right]\Biggr\}>0,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:c3}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_2:={}&\frac{\delta_1D}{2}
+\frac{(1-\delta_1Db)^2}
{b-\frac{\delta_1D(1+b^2)}{2}}\\
&+\frac{\delta_1^2(\kappa-Dq)^2}
{2\left[\delta_1q\left(\kappa-\frac{Dq}{2}\right)
-\frac{\delta_1^2b^2(Dq-\kappa)^2}
{b-\frac{\delta_1D(1+b^2)}{2}}\right]}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:C2}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\dot V_2(t)
\leq
-c_3E_u(t)
+
C_2\bigl(B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)\bigr)^2,
\label{eq:V2dot2}
\end{equation}
where $c_3$ and $C_2$ are independent of $\varepsilon$.

Since $X=Z-\varphi$,
\begin{align}
\bigl(B^\top P_\varepsilon X\bigr)^2
&\leq 2\bigl(B^\top P_\varepsilon Z\bigr)^2
 +2\bigl(B^\top P_\varepsilon\varphi\bigr)^2\nonumber\\
&\leq 2\abs{B}^2\abs{P_\varepsilon}V_1
 +2\abs{B}^2\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2\abs{\varphi}^2\nonumber\\
&\leq C_3\abs{P_\varepsilon}V_1
 +C_4\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2E_u,
\label{eq:sbound}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
C_3=2C_2\abs{B}^2,
\qquad
C_4=2C_2\abs{B}^2C_\varphi.
\label{eq:C34}
\end{equation}
Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\dot V_2
\leq
-c_3E_u
+
C_3\abs{P_\varepsilon}V_1
+
C_4\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2E_u.
\label{eq:V2dot3}
\end{equation}

\emph{Small-gain combination.}
Define
\begin{equation}
V(t)=V_1(t)+\varepsilon V_2(t).
\label{eq:V}
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:V1dot2} and \eqref{eq:V2dot3} gives
\begin{equation}
\dot V
\leq
-\varepsilon\bigl(1-C_3\abs{P_\varepsilon}\bigr)V_1
-
\left[
\varepsilon c_3
-(C_1+\varepsilon C_4)\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2
\right]E_u.
\label{eq:Vdot}
\end{equation}
By the low-gain property, there are $K_P>0$ and $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
\abs{P_\varepsilon}\leq K_P\varepsilon,
\qquad
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_0.
\label{eq:Pgain}
\end{equation}
It is sufficient to choose
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^*
=
\min\left\{
\varepsilon_0,\,
\frac{1}{2C_3K_P},\,
\frac{c_3}{4C_1K_P^2},\,
\sqrt{\frac{c_3}{4C_4K_P^2}}
\right\},
\label{eq:epsilon_star}
\end{equation}
with any zero-denominator restriction omitted. Then, for
$0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon^*$,
\begin{equation}
\dot V
\leq
-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}V_1
-\frac{\varepsilon c_3}{2}E_u.
\label{eq:Vdecay_pre}
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:V2_equiv},
\begin{equation}
\dot V
\leq
-\beta V,
\qquad
\beta
=
\min\left\{
\frac{\varepsilon}{2},
\frac{c_3}{2M_\delta}
\right\}>0.
\label{eq:Vdecay}
\end{equation}

\emph{Norm equivalence and well-posedness.}
From $X=Z-\varphi$ and \eqref{eq:phi_Eu}, there exist constants
$m_\varepsilon,M_\varepsilon>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
m_\varepsilon\Omega(t)
\leq
V(t)
\leq
M_\varepsilon\Omega(t).
\label{eq:equi}
\end{equation}
To make these bounds explicit, first note that
\begin{equation}
\abs{X}^2\leq 2\abs{Z}^2+2C_\varphi E_u,
\qquad
\abs{Z}^2\leq 2\abs{X}^2+2C_\varphi E_u,
\label{eq:norm_components}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\dint{\left(u_x^2(x,t)+u_t^2(x,t)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x}+u^2(D,t)
\leq 2(1+q^{-1})E_u(t).
\label{eq:wave_energy_upper}
\end{equation}
Since $V_1\geq\lambda_{\min}(P_\varepsilon)\abs{Z}^2$, these estimates give
\begin{equation}
\Omega(t)
\leq 2\lambda_{\min}(P_\varepsilon)^{-1}V_1(t)
 +\left[2C_\varphi+2(1+q^{-1})\right]E_u(t).
\label{eq:Omega_upper_V}
\end{equation}
As $V_1\leq V$ and $E_u\leq V_2/m_\delta\leq V/(\varepsilon m_\delta)$,
\begin{equation}
m_\varepsilon
=\left[2\lambda_{\min}(P_\varepsilon)^{-1}
+\frac{2C_\varphi+2(1+q^{-1})}{\varepsilon m_\delta}\right]^{-1}
\label{eq:m_epsilon}
\end{equation}
ensures $m_\varepsilon\Omega\leq V$.

Conversely, $V_1\leq\abs{P_\varepsilon}\abs{Z}^2$ and \eqref{eq:norm_components}
give
\begin{equation}
V_1\leq 2\abs{P_\varepsilon}\abs{X}^2
 +2C_\varphi\abs{P_\varepsilon}E_u,
\qquad
\varepsilon V_2\leq\varepsilon M_\delta E_u.
\label{eq:V_upper_components}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
E_u(t)&\leq\max\left\{\frac12,\frac q2\right\}
\left(\norm{u_x}_2^2+\norm{u_t}_2^2+u^2(D,t)\right)
\\
&\leq\max\left\{\frac12,\frac q2\right\}\Omega(t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Eu_Omega}
\end{equation}
Hence $V\leq M_\varepsilon\Omega$, where
\begin{equation}
M_\varepsilon
=2\abs{P_\varepsilon}
 +\left(2C_\varphi\abs{P_\varepsilon}+\varepsilon M_\delta\right)
\max\left\{\frac12,\frac q2\right\}.
\label{eq:M_epsilon}
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:equi} holds with the explicit constants in
\eqref{eq:m_epsilon} and \eqref{eq:M_epsilon}.
Gronwall's inequality applied to \eqref{eq:Vdecay} therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\Omega(t)
\leq
\frac{M_\varepsilon}{m_\varepsilon}
e^{-\beta t}\Omega(0),
\label{eq:stab_proof}
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:stab} with
$\alpha=M_\varepsilon/m_\varepsilon$.

Finally, the distributed maps
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
u&\longmapsto\dint{B_1(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x},\\
u_t&\longmapsto\dint{B_2(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bounded_couplings}
\end{equation}
are bounded from $H^1(0,D)$ and $L^2(0,D)$, respectively, into
$\mathbb{R}^n$. Together with the dissipative boundary feedback, this gives
the standard well-posedness of the closed-loop wave--ODE system and hence
existence and uniqueness of the stated energy solution. The higher-regularity
compatibility conditions in the theorem yield the corresponding classical
solution. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Nonexpansive ODE Extension}
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm3}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass3}, the closed-loop system with controller
\eqref{eq:critical_controller} is exponentially stable for all sufficiently
small $\varepsilon>0$ in the energy norm $\Omega$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The forwarding calculation \eqref{eq:Ztarget} is unchanged. In the
coordinates \eqref{eq:spectral_split}, write
$\widetilde Z=T^{-1}Z=(\widetilde Z_s,\widetilde Z_c)$ and
$\widetilde\varphi=T^{-1}\varphi=(\widetilde\varphi_s,\widetilde\varphi_c)$.
The closed-loop transformed equations are
\begin{align}
\dot{\widetilde Z}_c&=(A_c-B_cB_c^\top P_{c,\varepsilon})\widetilde Z_c
 +B_cB_c^\top P_{c,\varepsilon}\widetilde\varphi_c,
\label{eq:split_Zc}\\
\dot{\widetilde Z}_s&=A_s\widetilde Z_s
 -B_sB_c^\top P_{c,\varepsilon}
 (\widetilde Z_c-\widetilde\varphi_c).
\label{eq:split_Zs}
\end{align}

Let $P_s>0$ solve
$A_s^\top P_s+P_sA_s=-Q_s$ for any $Q_s>0$, and set
\begin{equation}
V_c=\widetilde Z_c^\top P_{c,\varepsilon}\widetilde Z_c,
\qquad
V_s=\widetilde Z_s^\top P_s\widetilde Z_s.
\label{eq:split_lyap}
\end{equation}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm1}, applied to the critical equation, gives
\begin{equation}
\dot V_c\leq-\varepsilon V_c
 +C_c\abs{P_{c,\varepsilon}}^2E_u.
\label{eq:split_Vc}
\end{equation}
Since $A_s$ is Hurwitz, Young's inequality applied to
\eqref{eq:split_Zs} gives constants $\lambda_s,C_s>0$ independent of
$\varepsilon$ such that
\begin{equation}
\dot V_s\leq-\lambda_sV_s
 +C_s\abs{P_{c,\varepsilon}}^2
 \left(\abs{\widetilde Z_c}^2+E_u\right).
\label{eq:split_Vs}
\end{equation}
The transformation estimate \eqref{eq:phi_Eu} remains valid because $T$ is
fixed. The wave estimate \eqref{eq:V2dot3} is also unchanged after replacing
$B^\top P_\varepsilon X$ by
$B_c^\top P_{c,\varepsilon}\widetilde X_c$.

Moreover, $P_{c,\varepsilon}/\varepsilon$ converges to a positive definite
matrix, so $\abs{P_{c,\varepsilon}}^2\abs{\widetilde Z_c}^2$ is bounded by a
constant times $\varepsilon V_c$. Consequently, for sufficiently small
$\varepsilon$, the composite functional
\begin{equation}
V=V_c+V_s+\varepsilon V_2
\label{eq:split_V}
\end{equation}
is equivalent to $\Omega$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}
\dot V\leq-c\left(\varepsilon V_c+V_s+\varepsilon E_u\right)
\leq-\beta V
\label{eq:split_decay}
\end{equation}
for some $c,\beta>0$. The norm-equivalence and well-posedness arguments are
the same as those following \eqref{eq:equi}. Hence
$\Omega(t)\leq\alpha e^{-\beta t}\Omega(0)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Nilpotent Case}
When all eigenvalues of $A$ are at the origin, the kernel problem simplifies and
an explicit endpoint gain is available. Let $g$ solve
\begin{equation}
g''(x)+B_1(x)=0,
\qquad
g'(0)=0,
\qquad
-g'(D)=qg(D),
\label{eq:nilpBVP}
\end{equation}
and set $g_0(x)=-B_2(x)$. Integrating the kernel equation gives
\begin{equation}
g'(x)=-\int_0^xB_1(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.
\label{eq:nilp_gprime}
\end{equation}
Evaluating at $x=D$ and using the right boundary condition yields
\begin{equation}
qg(D)=\dint{B_1(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}.
\label{eq:nilp_endpoint}
\end{equation}
Thus the effective endpoint gain is
\begin{equation}
\bar{B}=g(D)=\frac{1}{q}\dint{B_1(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}.
\label{eq:nilpB}
\end{equation}
Unlike the general case, this gain is obtained by direct integration and does
not require a matrix-valued boundary-value solve.

Define the nilpotent compensated state by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar{Z}(t)={}&X(t)-\dint{B_2(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}\\
&+\dint{g(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x}+b\bar{B}u(D,t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nilpZ}
\end{equation}
Using $g''+B_1=0$ and $B_2+g_0=0$ in the same integration-by-parts
calculation as above gives
\begin{equation}
\dot{\bar{Z}}(t)
=AX(t)+\bar{B}\bigl(u_x(D,t)+b\,u_t(D,t)+q\,u(D,t)\bigr).
\label{eq:nilpZdot}
\end{equation}
The corresponding controller is
\begin{equation}
U(t)=-\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon X(t)-b\,u_t(D,t)-q\,u(D,t),
\label{eq:nilpCtrl}
\end{equation}
where $P_\varepsilon$ is the positive definite solution of
\begin{equation}
A^\top P_\varepsilon+P_\varepsilon A
-P_\varepsilon\bar{B}\bar{B}^\top P_\varepsilon
=-\varepsilon P_\varepsilon.
\label{eq:nilp_are}
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass2}
All eigenvalues of $A$ are at the origin and the pair $(A,\bar{B})$ is
controllable, where $\bar{B}$ is given by \eqref{eq:nilpB}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm2}
Consider the closed-loop system consisting of \eqref{eq:ode},
\eqref{eq:wave}, \eqref{eq:left_boundary} and \eqref{eq:right_boundary}, and the controller
\eqref{eq:nilpCtrl} under Assumption~\ref{ass2}. Then there exists
$\varepsilon^*>0$ such that, for every $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^*]$ and
every initial state $(X(0),u(\cdot,0),u_t(\cdot,0))\in
\mathbb{R}^n\times H^1(0,D)\times L^2(0,D)$, the closed-loop system admits a
unique solution in
$C([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^n\times H^1(0,D)\times L^2(0,D))$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:stab}. If the initial data have the higher regularity and compatibility
properties stated in Theorem~\ref{thm1}, the corresponding solution is classical.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof follows the same Lyapunov construction as in Theorem~\ref{thm1};
the difference is the finite-dimensional estimate generated by the nilpotent
structure of $A$. From \eqref{eq:nilpZdot} and \eqref{eq:nilpCtrl},
\begin{equation}
\dot{\bar{Z}}
=\bigl(A-\bar{B}\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\bigr)X.
\label{eq:nilpZdot_feedback}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
\varphi:=\bar{Z}-X,
\qquad
V_1:=\bar{Z}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\bar{Z}.
\label{eq:nilp_vars}
\end{equation}
Since $X=\bar{Z}-\varphi$,
\begin{equation}
\dot{\bar{Z}}
=\bigl(A-\bar{B}\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\bigr)\bar{Z}
-\bigl(A-\bar{B}\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\bigr)\varphi.
\label{eq:nilpZdot2}
\end{equation}
Using the Riccati equation gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot V_1={}&-\varepsilon V_1
-\bigl(\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\bar{Z}\bigr)^2\\
&+2\bigl(\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\bar{Z}\bigr)
\bigl(\bar{B}^{\top}P_\varepsilon\varphi\bigr)
-2\bar{Z}^{\top}P_\varepsilon A\varphi.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nilV1_identity}
\end{equation}
The first three terms are handled by completing the square. For the last term,
Lemma~\ref{lem:lowgain}(3) gives
\begin{equation}
A^{\top}P_\varepsilon A
\preceq 3n^2\varepsilon^2P_\varepsilon.
\label{eq:nilp_riccati_bound}
\end{equation}
Young's inequality therefore yields
\begin{equation}
\dot V_1
\leq -\frac{\varepsilon}{2}V_1
+\left(\abs{\bar{B}}^2\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2
+6n^2\varepsilon\abs{P_\varepsilon}\right)\abs{\varphi}^2.
\label{eq:nilV1}
\end{equation}
The transformation error satisfies the same estimate as in the general case,
\begin{equation}
\abs{\varphi}^2\leq \bar C_\varphi E_u,
\label{eq:nilp_phi_Eu}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\bar C_\varphi
=3\norm{B_2}_2^2C_0+6\norm{g}_2^2
 +\frac{6b^2\abs{\bar B}^2}{q}.
\label{eq:nilp_Cphi}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\dot V_1
\leq -\frac{\varepsilon}{2}V_1
+\bar C_\varphi\left(\abs{\bar{B}}^2\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2
+6n^2\varepsilon\abs{P_\varepsilon}\right)E_u.
\label{eq:nilV1_Eu}
\end{equation}
By $\abs{P_\varepsilon}=O(\varepsilon)$, the positive coefficient in
\eqref{eq:nilV1_Eu} is $O(\varepsilon^2)$.

The wave functional $V_2$ in \eqref{eq:V2} and its equivalence estimate
\eqref{eq:V2_equiv} are unchanged, with $B$ replaced by $\bar B$ in the
boundary terms. The same calculation gives
\begin{equation}
\dot V_2
\leq -c_3E_u+\bar C_3\abs{P_\varepsilon}V_1
+\bar C_4\abs{P_\varepsilon}^2E_u,
\label{eq:nilV2}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\bar C_3=2C_2\abs{\bar B}^2,
\qquad
\bar C_4=2C_2\abs{\bar B}^2\bar C_\varphi,
\label{eq:nilp_C34}
\end{equation}
and the constants are independent of $\varepsilon$.
Define
\begin{equation}
V=V_1+\varepsilon V_2.
\label{eq:nilV}
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:nilV1_Eu} and \eqref{eq:nilV2}, and using
$\abs{P_\varepsilon}\leq K_P\varepsilon$, gives, for sufficiently small
$\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}
\dot V
\leq -\frac{\varepsilon}{4}V_1
-\frac{\varepsilon c_3}{2}E_u.
\label{eq:nilVdecay_pre}
\end{equation}
Since $V_2\leq M_\delta E_u$,
\begin{equation}
\dot V\leq-\beta_nV,
\qquad
\beta_n=\min\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{4},
\frac{c_3}{2M_\delta}\right\}>0.
\label{eq:nilVdecay}
\end{equation}
The norm-equivalence and well-posedness arguments are identical to those in
Theorem~\ref{thm1}, with $B$ replaced by $\bar B$. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\Omega(t)\leq\alpha_n e^{-\beta_n t}\Omega(0)
\label{eq:nilp_stab}
\end{equation}
for some $\alpha_n>0$. This proves exponential stability.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theoretical Boundary and Implementation Remarks}
\begin{remark}
Compared with backstepping designs for wave PDE--ODE cascades, the proposed
feedback uses only $X(t)$, $u(D,t)$, and $u_t(D,t)$ online. The full-domain
kernel is used offline to compute $B$ or $\bar{B}$. The price of this simpler
online structure is the spectral decomposition and the controllability
requirement on the critical pair in Assumption~\ref{ass3}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The condition $q<1/D$ is sufficient for the Lyapunov multiplier construction,
not a necessary condition for closed-loop stability. Likewise, the value
$\varepsilon^*$ in \eqref{eq:epsilon_star} is only a sufficient theoretical
small-gain bound. The analysis is carried out on the original wave state and
requires only the square-integrability assumption \eqref{eq:reg}; it can be
extended to other integral-bounded displacement couplings, including pointwise
coupling approximations, but not to unbounded velocity couplings. If $A$ has
right-half-plane eigenvalues, or if the critical pair $(A_c,B_c)$ is not
controllable, this low-gain design does not apply.
\end{remark}

\section{Numerical Simulation}
\label{sec:num}
All simulations run on $t \in [0,50]$ by a method-of-lines discretization of the
wave block. Introducing $v(x,t) := u_t(x,t)$ rewrites \eqref{eq:wave} as
\begin{equation}
u_t(x,t) = v(x,t),\qquad v_t(x,t) = u_{xx}(x,t).
\label{eq:mol}
\end{equation}
On the uniform mesh $x_j = j\Delta x$, $j = 0, \ldots, N$, with
\begin{equation}
N = 200,\qquad \Delta x = \frac{D}{N},\qquad \Delta t = 5 \times 10^{-4},
\label{eq:mesh}
\end{equation}
the PDE is semi-discretized by centered second differences, the boundary
conditions \eqref{eq:left_boundary} and \eqref{eq:right_boundary} are enforced by ghost points, and the
distributed coupling terms are evaluated by the composite trapezoidal rule. The
coupled ODE and semi-discrete PDE are advanced by a classical fourth-order
Runge--Kutta scheme, with $D = 1$, $\Delta x = 0.005$, and
$\Delta t/\Delta x = 0.1$. The effective gain $B$ in \eqref{eq:B} is computed by
the same trapezoidal rule on a fine quadrature grid; the result is insensitive
to the quadrature refinement, as reported in the examples.

\subsection{Example 1: General Marginally Stable Case}
\label{ex1}
Consider the cascade \eqref{eq:ode}--\eqref{eq:wave} with
\begin{equation}
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1\\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad D = 1,\qquad b = 1,\qquad q = 0.4,
\label{eq:ex1A}
\end{equation}
and smooth kernels
\begin{align}
B_1(x) &= e^{-30(x-0.7)^2}\begin{pmatrix} 1\\ 0.4 \end{pmatrix},\\
B_2(x) &= e^{-20(x-0.35)^2}\begin{pmatrix} 1\\ 0.25 \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:ex1B}
\end{align}
The profile $B_1$ is concentrated near the actuated boundary so that the
displacement channel regulates the oscillator directly, while $B_2$ is centered
in the interior to keep the velocity coupling genuinely distributed. With the
initial data $X(0) = (0.40,\,-0.2301)^{\top}$, $u(x,0) = 0.08\cos
\frac{\pi x}{2D}$, and $u_t(x,0) = 0$, the matrix $E$ in \eqref{eq:E} is
invertible, and \eqref{eq:B} gives
\begin{equation}
B = \begin{pmatrix} 0.2235\\ -0.5423 \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:ex1Bgain}
\end{equation}
Since the eigenvalues of $A$ are $\pm i$ and $\operatorname{rank}\left[B\;
AB\right] = 2$, Assumption~\ref{ass1} holds. Choosing the low-gain parameter
$\varepsilon = 0.12$, solving \eqref{eq:are} yields
\begin{equation}
P_{0.12} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.7314 & -0.0279\\ -0.0279 & 0.6688 \end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:ex1P}
\end{equation}
with $B^{\top}P_{0.12} = \left(0.1786,\; -0.3689\right)$, so the implemented
controller is
\begin{equation}
U(t) = -0.1786\,x_1(t) + 0.3689\,x_2(t) - u_t(D,t) - 0.4\,u(D,t).
\label{eq:ex1Ctrl}
\end{equation}

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{fig2.png}}
\caption{Closed-loop response for Example~\ref{ex1}: (a) ODE state trajectories
and (b) three-dimensional response of the wave displacement $u(x,t)$.}
\label{fig:ex1}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:ex1}(a) shows that both ODE states converge to the origin under
the controller \eqref{eq:ex1Ctrl}. Figure~\ref{fig:ex1}(b) further shows the
decay of the distributed actuator state. Hence, the complete PDE--ODE cascade is
stabilized in the general marginally stable case, in agreement with
Theorem~\ref{thm1}.

\subsection{Example 2: Nilpotent Chain}
\label{ex2}
To illustrate the simplified zero-eigenvalue design, consider the two-
dimensional chain
\begin{equation}
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1\\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad D = 1,\qquad b = 1,\qquad q = 0.5,
\label{eq:ex2A}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
B_1(x) = \begin{pmatrix} 0\\ 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad B_2(x) = 0.
\label{eq:ex2B}
\end{equation}
All eigenvalues of $A$ are at the origin, so this example falls into the
framework of \eqref{eq:nilpBVP}--\eqref{eq:nilpCtrl}. Since $q = 0.5$, the
explicit gain \eqref{eq:nilpB} becomes
\begin{equation}
\bar{B} = \frac{1}{q}\dint{B_1(x)\,\mathrm{d}x} =
\begin{pmatrix} 0\\ 2 \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:ex2Bbar}
\end{equation}
Choosing $\varepsilon = 0.20$ gives the Riccati solution of \eqref{eq:are}
\begin{equation}
P_{0.20} = \begin{pmatrix}
0.0020 & 0.0100\\
0.0100 & 0.1000
\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:ex2P}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\bar{B}^{\top}P_{0.20} =
\left(0.0200,\; 0.2000\right),
\label{eq:ex2Pt}
\end{equation}
so the implemented special-case controller is
\begin{equation}
U(t) = -\left(0.0200,\; 0.2000\right)X(t) - u_t(D,t) - 0.5\,u(D,t).
\label{eq:ex2Ctrl}
\end{equation}
The initial conditions are $X(0) = (0.30,\,-0.20)^{\top}$, $u(x,0) = 0.04\cos
\frac{\pi x}{2D}$, and $u_t(x,0) = 0$.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{fig3.png}}
\caption{Comparison for the nilpotent Example~\ref{ex2}: (a)--(b) ODE and wave
responses with boundary damping only; (c)--(d) ODE and three-dimensional wave
responses under the proposed low-gain controller.}
\label{fig:ex2}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:ex2} compares both the ODE and PDE responses with and without
the low-gain feedback term. Boundary damping alone cannot stabilize the
nilpotent ODE chain, whereas the proposed controller drives the ODE states and
the wave displacement to the origin. This confirms the effectiveness of the
simplified controller \eqref{eq:nilpCtrl} in the nilpotent case.

\subsection{Example 3: Effect of the Low-Gain Parameter}
\label{ex3}
We next use the general marginally stable plant and the initial data of
Example~\ref{ex1}, while varying the low-gain parameter as
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon\in\{0.2,\,0.5,\,0.9\}.
\label{eq:ex3eps}
\end{equation}
All remaining controller and discretization parameters are unchanged.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{fig4.png}}
\caption{Effect of the low-gain parameter in Example~\ref{ex3}. Each row
contains the ODE response (left) and the three-dimensional PDE response (right)
for (a)--(b) $\varepsilon=0.2$, (c)--(d) $\varepsilon=0.5$, and (e)--(f)
$\varepsilon=0.9$.}
\label{fig:ex3}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:ex3} illustrates the need to retain a sufficiently small
low-gain parameter. For this plant, $\varepsilon=0.2$ stabilizes both the ODE
states and the wave state, whereas the responses for $\varepsilon=0.5$ and
$\varepsilon=0.9$ grow. The more pronounced growth at $\varepsilon=0.9$ shows
that increasing $\varepsilon$ is not an unrestricted performance-tuning
mechanism. This behavior is consistent with the small-gain requirement in
Theorem~\ref{thm1}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}
This paper studied the low-gain stabilization problem for a class of wave
PDE--ODE cascade systems with distributed in-domain displacement and velocity
coupling. By a forwarding-based transformation the distributed input dynamics
reduce to a low-gain design problem with an explicit effective input vector
$B$, and the controller takes the simple form
$U(t) = -B^{\top}P_\varepsilon X(t) - b\,u_t(D,t) - q\,u(D,t)$, combining a
finite-dimensional low-gain term with boundary damping and stiffness; in the
nilpotent case the gain admits the explicit formula \eqref{eq:nilpB}. Under a
low-gain spectral assumption on the ODE dynamics, the closed-loop system is
exponentially stable in a natural energy norm, as proved through a Lyapunov
functional combining the Riccati-based ODE term with the damped wave energy and
suitable cross terms, with the analysis carried out directly on the original
wave state. In comparison with backstepping designs for wave actuator dynamics,
the proposed feedback is much simpler because it uses only the
finite-dimensional state and two local boundary traces online; the price is the
stronger open-loop spectral assumption. Numerical simulations confirm the
theoretical conclusions for both the general and the nilpotent settings. Future
work includes output-feedback designs, robustness with respect to modeling
uncertainty and disturbances, and extensions to more general hyperbolic
actuator dynamics or higher-dimensional distributed systems.
