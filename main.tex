\begin{abstract}
This article proposes two finite-dimensional controllers for wave PDE--ODE cascade systems with distributed displacement and velocity coupling. It is shown that the closed-loop system can be exponentially stabilized under suitable spectral and controllability conditions on the open-loop ODE plant. The proposed controllers are constructed using a forwarding transformation that converts the distributed coupling into an equivalent finite-dimensional boundary input channel. Compared with existing backstepping-based designs, the proposed controllers have a much simpler structure and require only the ODE state and local boundary measurements, rather than the full distributed wave state. However, additional spectral restrictions on the open-loop plant are imposed to achieve this simplification. Moreover, an even simpler controller is obtained when the eigenvalues of the ODE plant are all at the origin. Several numerical examples are provided to illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed controllers.

\end{abstract}

Boundary control, wave equation, distributed parameter systems, finite-dimensional control, PDE--ODE cascade systems.
\end{IEEEkeywords}



\section{Introduction}


Interconnections between ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and partial differential equations (PDEs) arise in various engineering systems involving flexible structures and mechanical vibrations \cite{krstic_compensating_2009,susto_control_2010,bekiaris-liberis_compensating_2010}. In particular, when the vibrations of a flexible structure are described by a wave equation and its distributed displacement and velocity affect a finite-dimensional subsystem, the resulting dynamics can be modeled as a wave PDE--ODE cascade with distributed coupling. In such systems, the control input may be applied at the boundary of the wave equation, whereas the ODE is driven by the wave state distributed over the spatial domain. In this context, we are concerned with the boundary stabilization of wave PDE--ODE cascades with distributed coupling.




Various approaches have been developed for the stabilization of PDE--ODE cascades. Backstepping has been widely employed to stabilize coupled PDE--ODE systems through infinite-dimensional transformations and boundary feedback design \cite{krstic_backstepping_2008,krstic_compensating_2009,susto_control_2010,bekiaris-liberis_compensating_2010}. More recently, Tsubakino and Krstic \cite{tsubakino_forwarding-based_2024} developed a forwarding-based controller for general linear ODE--wave cascades with distributed interconnections, allowing unstable ODE dynamics. However, these designs may require distributed PDE-state measurements and spatial integral terms in the feedback law. To overcome limitations in state availability, output-feedback controllers have also been developed using state observers and disturbance estimators \cite{than_output-feedback_2021,mei_infinite-dimensional_2023,feng_output-feedback_2024}. Although these methods reduce direct state-measurement requirements, the additional observer dynamics, particularly for infinite-dimensional systems, may increase the complexity of real-time implementation. These considerations motivate the development of stabilizing controllers with simpler online feedback structures.



To simplify the feedback implementation, Xu et al. \cite{xu_low-gain_2023} proposed a finite-dimensional feedback design for PDE--ODE cascades with distributed diffusion and counter convection. Their approach achieves exponential stabilization without requiring the full PDE state in the feedback law, at the expense of additional spectral restrictions on the ODE subsystem. This result motivates us to investigate whether a similar finite-dimensional control structure can be developed for wave PDE--ODE cascades. However, unlike diffusion equations, wave equations generally lack intrinsic energy dissipation and may possess nondecaying modes under certain boundary conditions. As a result, the stability analysis developed for diffusion PDE--ODE cascades cannot be directly applied to wave PDE--ODE cascades, since the dissipative properties underlying the original analysis are no longer available.



To address these challenges, this paper develops a finite-dimensional feedback controller for a linear ODE coupled with a one-dimensional wave equation through spatially distributed displacement and velocity terms. The wave equation satisfies a homogeneous Neumann boundary condition at $x=0$, with the control input applied at $x=D$. Motivated by existing boundary stabilization methods for wave equations \cite{krstic_output-feedback_2008,tsubakino_forwarding-based_2024}, we introduce boundary damping together with an additional stiffness feedback term to eliminate the nondecaying displacement mode and stabilize the wave subsystem. Following the finite-dimensional compensation idea in \cite{xu_low-gain_2023}, an auxiliary boundary-value problem is constructed to account for the distributed displacement and velocity coupling and determine an effective input vector $\mathfrak B$. The resulting controller takes the form
\begin{equation}
U(t)=-\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)-b\,u_t(D,t)-q\,u(D,t),
\label{eq:introcontroller}
\end{equation}
where $P_\varepsilon$ is the positive definite solution of a parametric algebraic Riccati equation. Unlike feedback designs requiring distributed wave-state information, the proposed controller uses only the ODE state and the wave displacement and velocity at the controlled boundary.

The main contributions of this paper, compared with relevant existing works, are summarized as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item To the best of our knowledge, this is the first attempt to develop a finite-dimensional feedback controller of the proposed structure for wave PDE--ODE cascades with spatially distributed displacement and velocity coupling. Compared with the forwarding-based design in \cite{tsubakino_forwarding-based_2024}, the proposed controller has a simpler form and does not require distributed wave-state integration in the online feedback. Unlike observer-based approaches \cite{than_output-feedback_2021,mei_infinite-dimensional_2023,feng_output-feedback_2024}, it introduces no additional observer dynamics. These advantages are obtained at the expense of more restrictive spectral conditions on the ODE subsystem.

\item A composite Lyapunov functional is developed to establish exponential stability under the proposed finite-dimensional feedback. The main difficulty lies in handling the multiple residual coupling terms arising from the distributed displacement and velocity interactions. To overcome this difficulty, the Riccati-based ODE energy is combined with the boundary-damped wave energy and suitable auxiliary terms. Through a careful selection of the functional weights and feedback parameters, the resulting cross terms are systematically estimated and absorbed into the dissipative terms, yielding a strict decay estimate for the coupled closed-loop system.

\item For the special case where the ODE matrix is nilpotent, the proposed controller admits a further simplified representation. By exploiting the nilpotent structure, the effective input vector can be expressed in a more explicit form, reducing the complexity of the offline controller design while preserving the finite-dimensional feedback structure. Numerical simulations are conducted to illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed controller and the exponential decay of the closed-loop states.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:prob} presents the problem formulation and preliminary results. Section~\ref{sec:stab} develops the proposed controllers and establishes exponential stability. Numerical examples are provided in Section~\ref{sec:num}, followed by conclusions in Section~\ref{sec:concl}.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prob}
\subsection{Problem Formulation}
In this article, we consider the following PDE--ODE cascade system:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot{X}(t)={}&AX(t)
+\int_0^D B_1(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm dx\\
&+\int_0^D B_2(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm dx
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ode}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
u_{tt}(x,t)=u_{xx}(x,t),
\label{eq:wave}
\end{equation}
with boundary conditions given as follows:
\begin{equation}
u_x(0,t)=0,
\label{eq:left_boundary}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
u_x(D,t)=U(t),
\label{eq:right_boundary}
\end{equation}
where $X(t)\in\mathbb{R}^n$ is the plant state,
$U(t)\in\mathbb{R}$ is the control signal,
$u(x,t):[0,D]\times[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$
is the actuator state, and
$B_1(x),B_2(x):[0,D]\to\mathbb{R}^n$
are distributed coupling functions satisfying
$\int_0^D |B_i(x)|^2\,\mathrm{d}x<+\infty$ for $i=1,2$.
The ODE subsystem is affected by the distributed
displacement and velocity of the wave, while the
wave equation describes the actuator dynamics.
The boundary conditions are of Neumann type,
with the control signal applied at $x=D$.


The objective is to exponentially stabilize the cascade using only the
ODE state $X(t)$ and the boundary measurements $u(D,t)$ and $u_t(D,t)$.

\subsection{Technical Lemmas}

The following lemmas provide useful Riccati properties
\cite{lin_low-gain_1999,xu_stabilization_2018,xu_semi-global_2019}
and Poincar\'e-type inequalities
\cite{krstic_smyshlyaev_boundary_2008}.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:riccati}
Assume $(A,B)$ is controllable and all eigenvalues of $A$ lie on the imaginary
axis. Then the parametric algebraic Riccati equation (ARE)
\begin{equation}
A^{\top}P + PA-PBB^{\top}P = -\varepsilon P
\label{eq:are}
\end{equation}
has a unique positive definite solution $P=P_\varepsilon>0$. Moreover, there
exist constants $K_P>0$ and $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
\|P_\varepsilon\|\leq K_P\varepsilon,
\qquad
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_0.
\label{eq:Pgain}
\end{equation}
If all eigenvalues of $A$ are at the origin, then, in addition,
\begin{equation}
A^{\top}P_\varepsilon A
\preceq 3n^2\varepsilon^2 P_\varepsilon.
\label{eq:riccati_nilp}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:poincare}
For every $y\in H^1(0,D)$,
\begin{equation}
\norm{y}_2^2
\leq 2D\,y^2(D)+D^2\norm{y_x}_2^2,
\label{eq:poincare}
\end{equation}
and
$\norm{y}_{L^\infty}^2
\leq 2y^2(D)+2D\norm{y_x}_2^2$.
\end{lemma}



\section{Main Results}
\label{sec:stab}

In this section, we propose two low-gain controllers for the system
\eqref{eq:ode}--\eqref{eq:right_boundary} under two different conditions
on the eigenvalues of the ODE matrix $A$.

Choose the boundary feedback parameters $b$ and $q$ such that
\begin{equation}
 b>0,\qquad 0<q<\frac{1}{D}.
\label{eq:parameterChoice}
\end{equation}
Define the effective input vector $\mathfrak B$ by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak B=E^{-1}\int_0^D\cosh(Ax)\bigl(AB_2(x)
             +B_1(x)\bigr)\,\mathrm dx.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:effectiveB}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 E=(bA+qI)\cosh(AD)+A\sinh(AD).
\label{eq:effectiveE}
\end{equation}
The matrix $E$ is assumed to be invertible.

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:marginal}
All eigenvalues of $A$ are on the imaginary axis, and the matrix pair
$(A,\mathfrak B)$ is controllable.
\end{assumption}

Under Assumption~\ref{ass:marginal}, we propose the following low-gain
controller:
\begin{equation}
U(t)=-\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)-b\,u_t(D,t)-q\,u(D,t),
\label{eq:controller}
\end{equation}
where $P_\varepsilon$ is the unique positive definite solution of the
following algebraic Riccati equation:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 A^\top P_\varepsilon+P_\varepsilon A
 -P_\varepsilon\mathfrak B\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon
 =-\varepsilon P_\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:main_are}
\end{equation}

Define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \Omega(t)={}&|X(t)|^2+u^2(D,t)\\
 &+\int_0^D\bigl(u_x^2(x,t)+u_t^2(x,t)\bigr)\,\mathrm dx.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
The following theorem shows the exponential stability of the closed-loop
system.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm1}
Consider the closed-loop system consisting of the ODE \eqref{eq:ode},
the wave equation \eqref{eq:wave}, the boundary conditions
\eqref{eq:left_boundary} and \eqref{eq:right_boundary}, and the controller
\eqref{eq:controller}, under Assumption~\ref{ass:marginal}.
Then there exists $\varepsilon^*>0$ such that, for every
$\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^*]$ and every initial state
$(X(0),u(\cdot,0),u_t(\cdot,0))\in
\mathbb R^n\times H^1(0,D)\times L^2(0,D)$, the closed-loop
system admits a unique solution
$(X(t),u(\cdot,t),u_t(\cdot,t))\in
C([0,\infty);\mathbb R^n\times H^1(0,D)\times L^2(0,D))$
satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \Omega(t)\leq\alpha e^{-\beta t}\Omega(0),\quad t\geq0,
\label{eq:stab}
\end{equation}
for some positive constants $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
If, in addition, $u(\cdot,0)\in H^2(0,D)$,
$u_t(\cdot,0)\in H^1(0,D)$, and the initial data satisfy the
boundary compatibility conditions of the closed-loop system,
then the corresponding solution is classical.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $g\in H^2(0,D;\mathbb R^n)$ be the solution of the boundary-value
problem
\begin{equation}
 g''(x)=A^2g(x)-AB_2(x)-B_1(x),
\label{eq:kernelODE}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 g'(0)=0,
\label{eq:kernelLeft}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 -g'(D)=(bA+qI)g(D).
\label{eq:kernelRight}
\end{equation}
It follows that
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\bigl[\cosh(Ax)g'(x)-A\sinh(Ax)g(x)\bigr]\\
&\quad=\cosh(Ax)\bigl(g''(x)-A^2g(x)\bigr)\\
&\quad=-\cosh(Ax)\bigl(AB_2(x)+B_1(x)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:kernelIdentity}
\end{equation}
Integrating over $[0,D]$ gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\int_0^D\cosh(Ax)\bigl(AB_2(x)+B_1(x)\bigr)\,\mathrm dx\\
&=-\bigl[\cosh(Ax)g'(x)-A\sinh(Ax)g(x)\bigr]_0^D\\
&=-\cosh(AD)g'(D)+A\sinh(AD)g(D)\\
&=\bigl[(bA+qI)\cosh(AD)+A\sinh(AD)\bigr]g(D)\\
&=Eg(D).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:kernelEndpointIdentity}
\end{equation}
Thus,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
g(D)&=E^{-1}\int_0^D\cosh(Ax)\bigl(AB_2(x)+B_1(x)\bigr)\,\mathrm dx\\
&=\mathfrak B.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:kernelEndpoint}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 Z(t)={}&X(t)+\int_0^D\bigl(Ag(x)-B_2(x)\bigr)u(x,t)\,\mathrm dx\\
 &+\int_0^Dg(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm dx+b\mathfrak B\,u(D,t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Z}
\end{equation}
Then, by \eqref{eq:ode}--\eqref{eq:right_boundary} and
\eqref{eq:kernelODE}--\eqref{eq:kernelEndpoint}, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot Z(t)={}&AX(t)+\int_0^D B_1(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm dx\\
 &+\int_0^D Ag(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm dx
  +\int_0^D g(x)u_{xx}(x,t)\,\mathrm dx\\
 &+b\mathfrak B\,u_t(D,t)\\
={}&AX(t)+\int_0^D\bigl(B_1(x)+g''(x)\bigr)u(x,t)\,\mathrm dx\\
 &+\int_0^D Ag(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm dx
  +\mathfrak B U(t)-g'(D)u(D,t)\\
 &+b\mathfrak B u_t(D,t)\\
={}&AZ(t)+\mathfrak B\bigl(U(t)+b\,u_t(D,t)+q\,u(D,t)\bigr)\\
={}&AZ(t)-\mathfrak B\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Zderivation}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
 \varphi(t)=Z(t)-X(t).
\label{eq:phi_def}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot Z(t)={}&(A-\mathfrak B\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon)Z(t)\\
 &+\mathfrak B\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon\varphi(t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Zdot2}
\end{equation}

Define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 E_u(t)={}&\frac12\int_0^D\bigl(u_x^2(x,t)+u_t^2(x,t)\bigr)\,\mathrm dx\\
 &+\frac q2u^2(D,t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Eu}
\end{equation}
It follows from \eqref{eq:wave}, \eqref{eq:right_boundary},
and \eqref{eq:controller} that
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot E_u(t)
={}&\int_0^D\bigl(u_x(x,t)u_{xt}(x,t)
 +u_t(x,t)u_{xx}(x,t)\bigr)\,\mathrm dx\\
 &+q\,u(D,t)u_t(D,t)\\
={}&u_t(D,t)\bigl(U(t)+q\,u(D,t)\bigr)\\
={}&-b\,u_t^2(D,t)-u_t(D,t)\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:EuDot}
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:Z}, \eqref{eq:phi_def}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:poincare},
we have
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
|\varphi(t)|^2\leq{}&3\|Ag-B_2\|_2^2\|u(\cdot,t)\|_2^2\\
 &+3\|g\|_2^2\|u_t(\cdot,t)\|_2^2
 +3b^2|\mathfrak B|^2u^2(D,t)\\
\leq{}&C_\varphi E_u(t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:phiBound}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 C_\varphi={}&12D(D+q^{-1})\|Ag-B_2\|_2^2+6\|g\|_2^2\\
 &+\frac{6b^2|\mathfrak B|^2}{q}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Cphi}
\end{equation}
The constant $C_\varphi$ is independent of $\varepsilon$.

Define
\begin{equation}
 V_1(t)=Z^\top(t)P_\varepsilon Z(t).
\label{eq:V1}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:main_are}, \eqref{eq:Zdot2}, and
\eqref{eq:phiBound},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot V_1(t)
={}&-\varepsilon V_1(t)
  -\bigl(\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon Z(t)\bigr)^2\\
 &+2\bigl(\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon Z(t)\bigr)
   \bigl(\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon\varphi(t)\bigr)\\
\leq{}&-\varepsilon V_1(t)
 +\bigl(\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon\varphi(t)\bigr)^2\\
\leq{}&-\varepsilon V_1(t)
 +C_\varphi|\mathfrak B|^2\|P_\varepsilon\|^2E_u(t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:V1dot2}
\end{equation}

Choose
\begin{equation}
 \kappa=\frac{1+Dq}{4},
\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
so that $Dq/2<\kappa<1/2$. For $\delta>0$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 V_2(t)={}&E_u(t)+\delta\int_0^D x\,u_x(x,t)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm dx\\
 &+\delta\kappa\int_0^D u(x,t)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm dx.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:V2}
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:poincare} and Young's inequality,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 |V_2(t)-E_u(t)|\leq{}&\delta\bigl[D+\kappa(2D(D+q^{-1})+1)\bigr]E_u(t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:V2difference}
\end{equation}
For sufficiently small $\delta$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac12 E_u(t)\leq V_2(t)\leq\frac32 E_u(t).
\label{eq:V2_equiv}
\end{equation}
Direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\int_0^D x\,u_x(x,t)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm dx\\
 &=\frac D2\bigl(u_x^2(D,t)+u_t^2(D,t)\bigr)\\
 &\quad-\frac12\int_0^D\bigl(u_x^2(x,t)+u_t^2(x,t)\bigr)\,\mathrm dx,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mult1}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\int_0^D u(x,t)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm dx
={}&\int_0^D\bigl(u_t^2(x,t)-u_x^2(x,t)\bigr)\,\mathrm dx\\
 &+u(D,t)u_x(D,t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mult2}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:EuDot}, \eqref{eq:mult1}, and
\eqref{eq:mult2},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot V_2(t)={}&-\frac{\delta(1+2\kappa)}2\|u_x(\cdot,t)\|_2^2
 -\frac{\delta(1-2\kappa)}2\|u_t(\cdot,t)\|_2^2\\
 &-\Bigl(b-\frac{\delta D(1+b^2)}2\Bigr)u_t^2(D,t)\\
 &-\delta q\Bigl(\kappa-\frac{Dq}{2}\Bigr)u^2(D,t)\\
 &+\delta b(Dq-\kappa)u(D,t)u_t(D,t)\\
 &-(1-\delta Db)u_t(D,t)\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)\\
 &+\delta(Dq-\kappa)u(D,t)\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)\\
 &+\frac{\delta D}{2}(\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t))^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:V2dot}
\end{equation}
By Young's inequality,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\delta b|Dq-\kappa|\,|u(D,t)u_t(D,t)|\\
&\quad\leq\frac b4u_t^2(D,t)
 +\delta^2b(Dq-\kappa)^2u^2(D,t),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:young1}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&|1-\delta Db|\,|u_t(D,t)\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)|\\
&\quad\leq\frac b4u_t^2(D,t)
 +\frac{(1-\delta Db)^2}{b}(\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t))^2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:young2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\delta|Dq-\kappa|\,|u(D,t)\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)|\\
&\quad\leq\frac{\delta q}{2}\Bigl(\kappa-\frac{Dq}{2}\Bigr)u^2(D,t)\\
&\qquad+\frac{\delta(Dq-\kappa)^2}{2q(\kappa-Dq/2)}
 (\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t))^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:young3}
\end{equation}
Take $\delta$ small enough to satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \delta\bigl[D+\kappa\bigl(2D(D+q^{-1})+1\bigr)\bigr]\leq\frac12,
\label{eq:delta1}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\delta D(1+b^2)}2\leq\frac b4,
\label{eq:delta2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \delta b(Dq-\kappa)^2\leq
     \frac q4\Bigl(\kappa-\frac{Dq}2\Bigr).
\label{eq:delta3}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \dot V_2(t)\leq-cE_u(t)
   +C(\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t))^2,
\label{eq:V2dot2}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 c=\min\Bigl\{\delta(1+2\kappa),\,\delta(1-2\kappa),\,
 \frac\delta2\Bigl(\kappa-\frac{Dq}{2}\Bigr)\Bigr\}>0,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:constc}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 C={}&\frac{\delta D}{2}+\frac{(1-\delta Db)^2}{b}
 +\frac{\delta(Dq-\kappa)^2}{2q(\kappa-Dq/2)}>0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:constC}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:phiBound}, \eqref{eq:V1}, and
\eqref{eq:phi_def},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 (\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t))^2
 \leq{}&2(\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon Z(t))^2
      +2(\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon\varphi(t))^2\\
 \leq{}&2|\mathfrak B|^2\|P_\varepsilon\|V_1(t)\\
 &+2C_\varphi|\mathfrak B|^2\|P_\varepsilon\|^2E_u(t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:feedbackBound}
\end{equation}
Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot V_2(t)\leq{}&2C|\mathfrak B|^2\|P_\varepsilon\|V_1(t)\\
 &-\bigl(c-2CC_\varphi|\mathfrak B|^2\|P_\varepsilon\|^2\bigr)E_u(t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:V2dot3}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
 V(t)=V_1(t)+\varepsilon V_2(t).
\label{eq:V}
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:V1dot2} and \eqref{eq:V2dot3} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot V(t)\leq{}&-\varepsilon\bigl(1-2C|\mathfrak B|^2\|P_\varepsilon\|\bigr)V_1(t)\\
 &-\bigl[\varepsilon c-C_\varphi|\mathfrak B|^2
   (1+2\varepsilon C)\|P_\varepsilon\|^2\bigr]E_u(t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Vdot}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:Pgain}, for sufficiently small
$\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \dot V(t)&\leq-\frac\varepsilon2V_1(t)
                  -\frac{\varepsilon c}2E_u(t)\\
 &\leq-\frac\varepsilon2V_1(t)
                  -\frac{\varepsilon c}3V_2(t)\\
 &\leq-\beta V(t),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Vdecay}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \beta=\min\Bigl\{\frac\varepsilon2,\frac c3\Bigr\}>0.
\label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}

Furthermore,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 |X(t)|^2&\leq2|Z(t)|^2+2C_\varphi E_u(t),\\
 |Z(t)|^2&\leq2|X(t)|^2+2C_\varphi E_u(t),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:normRelations}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{\min}(P_\varepsilon)|Z(t)|^2
\leq V_1(t)\leq\|P_\varepsilon\||Z(t)|^2.
\label{eq:normP}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:Eu}, \eqref{eq:V2_equiv}, and
\eqref{eq:normRelations}--\eqref{eq:normP},
\begin{equation}
 m_\varepsilon\Omega(t)\leq V(t)\leq M_\varepsilon\Omega(t),
\label{eq:energyEquiv}
\end{equation}
where the positive constants can be chosen as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
m_\varepsilon&=\min\Bigl\{\frac{\lambda_{\min}(P_\varepsilon)}{2},\,
 \frac{\varepsilon}{4(C_\varphi+\max\{1,q^{-1}\})}\Bigr\},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:lowerBoundConstant}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
M_\varepsilon=\max\Bigl\{2\|P_\varepsilon\|,\,
\max\{1,q\}\Bigl(C_\varphi\|P_\varepsilon\|+\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\Bigr)\Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:upperBoundConstant}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \Omega(t)\leq\frac{M_\varepsilon}{m_\varepsilon}
 e^{-\beta t}\Omega(0),\qquad t\geq0.
\label{eq:stab_proof}
\end{equation}
The well-posedness argument is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:wellposed}. The calculations above first apply to
compatible classical solutions and extend to all energy solutions by
density. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

Next, we consider the case where all eigenvalues of $A$ are at the
origin. In this case, a simpler effective input vector can be obtained.
Define
\begin{equation}
 \bar B=\frac1q\int_0^D B_1(x)\,\mathrm dx.
\label{eq:nilpB}
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:nilp}
All eigenvalues of $A$ are at the origin, and the matrix pair
$(A,\bar B)$ is controllable.
\end{assumption}

Under Assumption~\ref{ass:nilp}, we propose
\begin{equation}
U(t)=-\bar B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t)-b\,u_t(D,t)-q\,u(D,t),
\label{eq:nilpCtrl}
\end{equation}
where $P_\varepsilon>0$ is the solution of
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 A^\top P_\varepsilon+P_\varepsilon A
 -P_\varepsilon\bar B\bar B^\top P_\varepsilon
 =-\varepsilon P_\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nilp_are}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm2}
Consider the closed-loop system consisting of \eqref{eq:ode},
\eqref{eq:wave}, \eqref{eq:left_boundary},
\eqref{eq:right_boundary}, and controller \eqref{eq:nilpCtrl}, under
Assumption~\ref{ass:nilp}. Then there exists $\varepsilon_n^*>0$
such that for every $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_n^*]$ and initial state
$(X(0),u(\cdot,0),u_t(\cdot,0))\in
\mathbb R^n\times H^1(0,D)\times L^2(0,D)$, the closed-loop
system admits a unique solution
$(X(t),u(\cdot,t),u_t(\cdot,t))\in
C([0,\infty);\mathbb R^n\times H^1(0,D)\times L^2(0,D))$
satisfying \eqref{eq:stab} for some positive
constants $\alpha$ and $\beta$. For compatible initial data with
$u(\cdot,0)\in H^2(0,D)$ and $u_t(\cdot,0)\in H^1(0,D)$,
the solution is classical.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $g\in H^2(0,D;\mathbb R^n)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
 g''(x)=-B_1(x),
\label{eq:nilpKernelODE}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 g'(0)=0,
\label{eq:nilpKernelLeft}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 -g'(D)=qg(D).
\label{eq:nilpKernelRight}
\end{equation}
Integrating \eqref{eq:nilpKernelODE} and using
\eqref{eq:nilpKernelLeft}--\eqref{eq:nilpKernelRight} yields
\begin{equation}
 g(D)=\frac1q\int_0^D B_1(x)\,\mathrm dx=\bar B.
\label{eq:nilpKernelEndpoint}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 Z(t)={}&X(t)-\int_0^D B_2(x)u(x,t)\,\mathrm dx\\
 &+\int_0^D g(x)u_t(x,t)\,\mathrm dx+b\bar B\,u(D,t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nilpZ}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot Z(t)={}&AX(t)+\bar B\bigl(U(t)+b\,u_t(D,t)+q\,u(D,t)\bigr)\\
={}&(A-\bar B\bar B^\top P_\varepsilon)X(t)\\
={}&(A-\bar B\bar B^\top P_\varepsilon)Z(t)\\
 &-(A-\bar B\bar B^\top P_\varepsilon)\varphi(t),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nilpZdot}
\end{equation}
where, for the present construction,
\begin{equation}
 \varphi(t)=Z(t)-X(t).
\label{eq:nilpPhi}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
 V_1(t)=Z^\top(t)P_\varepsilon Z(t).
\label{eq:nilpV1}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:nilp_are} and Lemma~\ref{lem:riccati},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot V_1(t)={}&-\varepsilon V_1(t)
 -(\bar B^\top P_\varepsilon Z(t))^2\\
 &+2(\bar B^\top P_\varepsilon Z(t))
 (\bar B^\top P_\varepsilon\varphi(t))\\
 &-2Z^\top(t)P_\varepsilon A\varphi(t)\\
\leq{}&-\frac\varepsilon2V_1(t)
 +\bigl(|\bar B|^2\|P_\varepsilon\|^2
 +6n^2\varepsilon\|P_\varepsilon\|\bigr)|\varphi(t)|^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nilV1_identity}
\end{equation}
As in \eqref{eq:phiBound},
\begin{equation}
 |\varphi(t)|^2\leq\bar C_\varphi E_u(t),
\label{eq:nilp_phiBound}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar C_\varphi={}&12D(D+q^{-1})\|B_2\|_2^2+6\|g\|_2^2
 +\frac{6b^2|\bar B|^2}{q}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nilp_Cphi}
\end{equation}
For the wave part, \eqref{eq:V2} and
\eqref{eq:mult1}--\eqref{eq:constC} give
\begin{equation}
 \dot V_2(t)\leq-cE_u(t)
  +C(\bar B^\top P_\varepsilon X(t))^2.
\label{eq:nilV2}
\end{equation}
Proceeding as in \eqref{eq:feedbackBound} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot V_2(t)\leq{}&2C|\bar B|^2\|P_\varepsilon\|V_1(t)\\
 &-\bigl(c-2C\bar C_\varphi|\bar B|^2\|P_\varepsilon\|^2\bigr)E_u(t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nilpV2bound}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
 V(t)=V_1(t)+\varepsilon V_2(t).
\label{eq:nilpV}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:nilV1_identity}, \eqref{eq:nilp_phiBound}, and
\eqref{eq:nilpV2bound}, for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \dot V(t)&\leq-\frac\varepsilon4V_1(t)
                 -\frac{\varepsilon c}2E_u(t)\\
          &\leq-\beta_n V(t),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nilVdecay}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \beta_n=\min\Bigl\{\frac\varepsilon4,\frac c3\Bigr\}>0.
\label{eq:betaN}
\end{equation}
The equivalence of $V(t)$ and $\Omega(t)$ and the well-posedness
argument follow as in Theorem~\ref{thm1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:online}
The functions $g$ and $Z$ are used only in the stability proof.
The controllers \eqref{eq:controller} and \eqref{eq:nilpCtrl} require
only $X(t)$, $u(D,t)$, and $u_t(D,t)$ for online implementation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:lowgain}
The low-gain property in Lemma~\ref{lem:riccati} implies
$\|P_\varepsilon\|=O(\varepsilon)$.
The estimate \eqref{eq:Vdecay} shows that small values of
$\varepsilon$ may lead to slow convergence. The theoretical bound
$\varepsilon^*$ is sufficient and can be conservative.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:scope}
Assumption~\ref{ass:marginal} does not require $A$ to be
diagonalizable. Thus it includes ODEs with integrator chains as well as
oscillators. The restriction on the eigenvalues is needed for the
present low-gain design, while full-state forwarding and backstepping
methods can handle broader ODE dynamics at the cost of more complicated
online feedback \cite{tsubakino_forwarding-based_2024}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:offline}
The matrix functions in \eqref{eq:effectiveB} and
\eqref{eq:effectiveE} are defined by their usual power series, so
neither $A^{-1}$ nor a diagonalization of $A$ is needed.
In fact, under Assumption~\ref{ass:marginal} and
\eqref{eq:parameterChoice}, $E$ is invertible.
For each eigenvalue $\lambda=i\omega$ of $A$, where
$\omega\in\mathbb R$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &(b\lambda+q)\cosh(\lambda D)+\lambda\sinh(\lambda D)\\
 &\quad=(q+ib\omega)\cos(\omega D)-\omega\sin(\omega D)\neq0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Enonsingular}
\end{equation}
Indeed, for $\omega=0$ the value is $q>0$, while for
$\omega\neq0$ its real and imaginary parts cannot both be zero.
Thus the matrix $E$ is nonsingular for all matrices satisfying
Assumption~\ref{ass:marginal}.
\end{remark}

trapezoidal rule. All examples use $D=1$, $t\in[0,50]$, and

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}
This paper developed finite-dimensional boundary feedback for wave PDE--ODE
cascades with distributed displacement and velocity coupling. An explicit
offline effective input vector leads to a controller that combines
Riccati-based low-gain ODE compensation with wave boundary damping and
stiffness. Exponential stability was established through a forwarding
transformation and a strict composite Lyapunov estimate. A separate
compensation kernel yields a simpler effective input formula for the
nilpotent ODE case. Both feedback laws use only the ODE state and two
boundary measurements online, avoiding spatial wave-state integration at
the cost of a spectral restriction on the ODE dynamics. Future work will
consider broader ODE spectra and output-feedback implementations.

\section{Well-Posedness of the Closed Loop}
\label{app:wellposed}
Here we justify the semigroup step used in Theorems~\ref{thm1} and
\ref{thm2}. Write $v=u_t$ and $K=\mathfrak B^\top P_\varepsilon$ or
$K=\bar B^\top P_\varepsilon$, respectively. Equip
$\mathcal H=\mathbb R^n\times H^1(0,D)\times L^2(0,D)$ with the equivalent
Hilbert norm
\begin{equation}
 \|(X,f,v)\|_{\mathcal H}^2
 =|X|^2+\|f'\|_2^2+\|v\|_2^2+qf^2(D).
\label{eq:semigroupNorm}
\end{equation}
The closed-loop operator is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal G_K(X,f,v)=\bigl(&AX+\textstyle\int_0^D B_1f\,\mathrm dx
                  +\int_0^D B_2v\,\mathrm dx,\\
 &v,\ f''\bigr)
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:generator}
\end{equation}
on the domain
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 D(\mathcal G_K)=\bigl\{(X,f,v):\;&X\in\mathbb R^n,\;f\in H^2(0,D),\\
 &v\in H^1(0,D),\;f'(0)=0,\\
 &f'(D)+bv(D)+qf(D)=-KX\bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:generatorDomain}
\end{equation}
To reduce this domain to a homogeneous boundary condition, take
$\chi(x)=-x^2/[D(2+qD)]$, which satisfies $\chi'(0)=0$ and
$\chi'(D)+q\chi(D)=-1$. The bounded invertible change of coordinates
$(X,f,v)\mapsto(X,w,v)$, $w=f-\chi KX$, converts the boundary condition
into $w'(0)=0$ and $w'(D)+bv(D)+qw(D)=0$.
In these coordinates, the operator differs by bounded maps on
$\mathcal H$ from the direct sum of the finite-dimensional generator $A$
and the standard wave generator with positive boundary damping $b$
and stiffness $q$. Indeed, the distributed maps from $H^1\times L^2$
to $\mathbb R^n$ are bounded, while $\chi$, $\chi''$ and the fixed row
vector $K$ yield bounded additional state-space terms.
The homogeneous wave generator is maximal dissipative in the wave-energy
inner product: dissipativity follows by integration by parts, and
surjectivity of its positive resolvent follows from the coercive
Robin variational problem. The bounded perturbation theorem and similarity
then show that $\mathcal G_K$ generates a $C_0$-semigroup on $\mathcal H$.
Classical compatible initial data form a dense subset of $\mathcal H$;
therefore the Lyapunov estimates extend to all energy solutions.
